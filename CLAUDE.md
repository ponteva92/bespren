# Bespren — Game Design Document & Technical Direction

**Status:** Mobile LAN world-map, salvage, auto-aim, and defense-combat vertical slice, Godot 4.7.1 Mobile  
**Target:** Android, 480×270 logical landscape viewport, local two-player co-op  
**Art direction:** AAA+ post-apocalyptic survival readability, authored for constrained mobile rendering  
**Document role:** Living product contract for design, engineering, art, and validation. The measurement record behind sections 7-9 lives in [`docs/art-log/`](docs/art-log/README.md); this file states what ships and the rules learned.

## 1. Product vision

Bespren is a top-down survival action RPG about two scavengers holding a fragile technological refuge together after infrastructure and ecology have collapsed. Its emotional center is the Base Core: a warm, rhythmic signal in a world whose rust, rot, rain, and oxidized machinery constantly communicate loss. The game should make cooperation feel physical and legible even on a small shared screen, with Heikki and Shane reading as complementary silhouettes before the player notices individual equipment.

The long-term thirty-second loop is **scan → salvage → return → reinforce → survive**. The current vertical slice supplies a character-select boot menu, safe-area-aware multitouch movement and action triggers, a 28,672-unit multi-biome world, host-resolved static collisions, deterministic three-to-five-step host-authoritative resource gathering with unchanged one-unit node yield, a replicated shared resource pool, the 450-second day/night clock and zombie horde, eight Tier-1 construction cards, three automatic towers, three functional traps, 64px 2D placement, transactional Base relocation, weighted zombie auto-aim, visible host-approved projectiles, eight distinct enemy presentations, pooled action/UI audio, ENet transport, automatic Solo fallback, and validation harness. Base deposit, persistent inventory, long-term progression, and device certification remain outside the implemented scope.

## 2. Design pillars

**The refuge is a character.** The Base Core is always warmer, rounder, and more rhythmic than the world around it. Its Amber Gold PointLight2D and aura establish safety without requiring dialogue, while any future state change must remain readable through pulse cadence, value, and shape rather than color alone.

**Two players, two signatures.** Heikki owns a broad, stable silhouette with Gold utility accents; Shane owns a narrower, asymmetric silhouette with Cyan/Teal technology accents. Local co-op must preserve independent input ownership and never infer that two actions from one device represent two players.

**Decay tells history.** Rust is granular and hot, rotten wood has directional grain and dark cavities, and oxidized copper blooms in cool organic islands. Weathering may enrich surfaces, but silhouettes, interaction edges, and navigation-critical values must remain cleaner than decorative background regions.

**Mobile clarity is the quality bar.** “AAA+” here means coherent art direction, disciplined hierarchy, reproducible assets, controlled effects, and strong validation—not desktop-only post-processing or excessive texture resolution. Every visual choice must survive the 480×270 logical target, mid-range Android bandwidth limits, touch occlusion, and sustained thermal load.

## 3. Audience, format, and session promise

The intended audience values compact cooperative survival sessions, visible base growth, scavenging tension, and expressive character roles. The current playable format is one local touch player per Android device with Solo, Host LAN, and Join LAN launch paths; keyboard input remains available for desktop development. The earlier two-seat shared-screen roster remains a validated data contract, but the shipping runtime slice now exercises peer-one authority through either ENet on UDP 8791 or `OfflineMultiplayerPeer` fallback.

Sessions should ultimately support short ten-minute salvage runs and longer fortification arcs without requiring an always-on connection. `OfflineMultiplayerPeer` makes the local SceneTree authoritative at peer ID 1, and the ENet runtime preserves that same authority boundary on UDP 8791. Two-process desktop loopback probes now cover movement, actions, host-selected resource gathering, replicated depletion, and replicated inventory; physical two-device Android LAN behavior remains a separate release gate.

## 4. Core loop and progression contract

The implemented moment-to-moment slice lets players read the world, move between regions, identify Wood, Metal, and Tech, and gather through the host-validated `[USE]` path. Each node accepts a deterministic three-to-five interactions, grants nothing on intermediate steps, and grants the same single resource unit only on completion; replicated progress and late-join snapshots preserve that partial work. Gather commits feed the shared construction pool. During the 450-second day, players may inspect and place any affordable Tier-1 structure; night begins automatically or through a host-validated command. Kinetic, Chemical, and Electric towers acquire zombies only inside their configured ranges and apply direct, area, or chain damage. Landmine, Slowing Pit, and Razor Snare implement single-use blast, persistent slow, and reusable damage/root behavior respectively. Traps remain placeable structures but do not block player motion or flow-field navigation.

Long-term progression should combine player mastery, persistent refuge upgrades, character-specific tools, and access to new regions. Resource colors are already reserved as semantic anchors: Wood uses `#FFD700`, Metal uses `#E0E0E0`, and Tech uses `#00FFFF`. Future UI, loot feedback, and upgrade previews must reuse these anchors while adding icons, patterns, and labels for color-vision accessibility.

## 5. Technical architecture

The application boots through `res://scenes/ui/StartMenu.tscn`; it selects Heikki or Shane and configures `res://scenes/game/game_world.tscn` before that scene enters the tree. `GameWorld` composes `CoopSession`, `DayNightCycle`, `TacticalBuildSystem`, `GridPlacementController2D`, `BesprenWorldMap2D`, `BesprenResourceScatter2D`, the Y-sorted player/projectile/defense roots, Base Core presentation, `JuiceRig`, `GameAudioController`, `GameHUD`, and `MobileControls`, while the earlier `bespren_foundation.tscn` remains the visual/local-roster validation composition. Gameplay domains must continue to arrive as composed child systems or typed Resources rather than being folded into the menu or bootstrap.

The architecture uses explicit Godot types, `class_name`, typed arrays, typed signals, enums, node-path contracts, and Resource-backed seat definitions. Cross-domain behavior should communicate through narrow signals or validated commands; nodes must not reach through unrelated scene branches. Autoloads are intentionally absent because no system in this milestone requires process-global lifetime. `GameSettings` follows the same rule: a typed object the StartMenu and `GameWorld` each load from `user://settings.cfg`, not a singleton. The world's skeleton is data: `BesprenWorldMap2D` preloads `res://data/world/world_composition.tres` (`WorldCompositionContract`), validates it at build, and reads the camp seat and satellites, road routes with their grades, biome paint, landmarks, and every dress layer's pocket table from it. `STARTING_CAMP_POSITION` and the layers' `*_POCKETS` names are static accessors that resolve through that file. Never author a second copy.

```text
res://
├── assets/2d/                 # Curated runtime art only
│   ├── actors/                # Baked 8-direction actor sheets, SpriteFrames, scenes
│   ├── characters/            # Blender-rendered Heikki and Shane sprites
│   ├── effects/               # Base Core and radial light sources
│   ├── environment/           # Offline-baked collidable environment sprites
│   └── resources/             # Wood, Metal, Tech vector masters
├── data/coop/                 # Reserved for authored seat/profile Resources
├── data/world/                # WorldCompositionContract: camp, roads, districts, landmarks, pockets
├── scenes/
│   ├── characters/            # Presentation-only character scenes
│   ├── main/                  # Composition root
│   ├── resources/             # Presentation-only resource scenes
│   ├── showcase/              # Integrated art-direction target
│   └── world/                 # Base Core visual scene
├── shaders/                   # Mobile CanvasItem shaders
├── src/
│   ├── coop/                  # Seat ownership and authority boundary
│   ├── core/                  # Application composition
│   ├── feedback/              # Impact camera, hit VFX, and juice without game rules
│   └── visual/                # Presentation drivers without game rules
├── tests/                     # Headless and GPU render gates

├── tools/ci/                  # Gate runner shared by workstations and CI

```

The root-level `Addons/` directory is an external source-asset vault and contains many independent projects and third-party packages. `Addons/.gdignore` prevents Godot from treating that archive as production `res://` content, while no source asset has been deleted. `tools/asset_pipeline/audit_and_extract_runtime_assets.py` recursively inventories the vault, fully decompresses ZIP members for path/CRC/safety validation, promotes only reviewed runtime art/audio and license files, verifies copied hashes, and writes `assets/runtime_asset_manifest.json`. Any later promotion must preserve that provenance and scene-level reachability.

## 6. Local co-op and authority contract

`PlayerSlotDefinition` describes a seat using an ID, display name, control scheme, device ID, and identity color. `LocalCoopRoster` owns at most two unique seats, rejects duplicate joypad ownership, routes physical events to a seat, and returns defensive copies of its typed array. The default contract is Heikki on touch/keyboard and Shane on joypad device 1.

`CoopSession` starts Solo, ENet Host, or ENet Client sessions on UDP 8791. Replaceable movement requests and snapshots use `unreliable_ordered`; registration and Interact/Fire commits remain `reliable`. Clients address peer 1 with `rpc_id(1)`, the host validates character IDs, registered peers, finite unit movement vectors, interaction cooldowns, and the two allowed action identifiers, then integrates and rebroadcasts authoritative `Vector2` state at 20 Hz. Accepted fire commits include the host-recorded facing direction so every peer renders the same projectile. A failed or disconnected client automatically replaces its peer with `OfflineMultiplayerPeer`, preserving the exact same peer-one authority path.

`AutoAimController2D` derives candidates from the bounded host-authoritative horde registry and an `Area2D`, then scores distance, aim angle, threat, and continuity instead of choosing nearest-only. A 150-degree cone, line-of-sight invalidation, lock hysteresis, minimum lock time, and smoothed presentation prevent target flicker while keeping the server's final fire direction authoritative. Invalid or occluded locks are synchronously rejected again at fire resolution.

`MobileControls` owns independent finger indices for movement, Interact, and Fire, consumes every owned UI touch before unhandled world input, quantizes the left stick to eight directions, and exposes two 44×44 logical action targets. Safe-area insets are converted from display coordinates into the 480×270 logical canvas before control placement. A second local touch player must not be added by reusing global `Input` polling; it requires another explicit seat-scoped provider and conflict map.

Godot’s `InputEvent.device` property provides the physical device identifier used by the current router, and Godot supports multiple simultaneous controllers. The offline peer contract intentionally mirrors a server with no connected peers, for which `is_server()` is true and the unique ID is the server target. These choices follow the official [InputEvent](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_inputevent.html), [controller](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/inputs/controllers_gamepads_joysticks.html), and [OfflineMultiplayerPeer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_offlinemultiplayerpeer.html) documentation.

## 7. Scene contracts

Presentation reports the simulation and never decides it. Each class below owns what it names and nothing more. The measurement record behind every shipped value here - how it was found, what was refuted, and which instruments misled first - is [`docs/art-log/07-scene-contracts.md`](docs/art-log/07-scene-contracts.md).

**Base Core.** `base_core.tscn` is a presentation scene: a Blender-authored survivor camp (canvas tent, fire pit, workbench, crates, radio mast, sandbag perimeter), an Amber Gold `PointLight2D`, a restrained radial aura, an additive scale-glow silhouette, and `CorePulseDriver`, which owns the exact 1.15× command scale-punch and the pulse only - no health, build radius, inventory, or defensive authority. `game_world.tscn` instances it at `Vector2(2, 2)`. Future Base mechanics compose beside this view rather than turning the driver into a game-state authority.

**Resource nodes.** The Wood, Metal, and Tech scenes each carry a mipmapped SVG icon drawn with `TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS`, a pulsing `toon_emissive_outline` material whose outline is an identity rim in the resource's own anchor colour at `outline_width = 1.4`, a visibility-gated identity light, and absolute gameplay-pass Z. `ResourceNodeView` presents authoritative partial-harvest feedback through bounded scale, alpha, glow, and hit response, and owns no quantity, regeneration, ownership, or pickup decision. `BesprenResourceScatter2D` owns deterministic IDs, three-to-five-step durability, per-peer focus locks, range validation, totals, ordered progress revisions, final depletion, and late-join snapshots.

**Survivors.** Gameplay draws the baked directional actors `assets/2d/actors/scenes/hero_*.tscn` through `PlayerAvatar` at `BAKED_ACTOR_VISUAL_SCALE = 1.5`. `heikki.tscn` and `shane.tscn` (Blender RGBA sprites, shared toon outline at `1.6`, separate accent lights) are reached only through `force_legacy_presentation`, and their PNGs ship otherwise only on the StartMenu cards - confirm which path draws an asset before measuring it. `CharacterPresentation` duplicates its material before applying an accent. `network_player.tscn` wraps the presentation in a radius-9 `CharacterBody2D` on Players/WorldStatic filters, follows authoritative `Vector2` state through `move_and_slide()`, and switches Idle/Run from actual velocity. Equipment, abilities, and damage state remain absent.

**HUD and tactical.** `GameHUD` owns the `148 × 58` layer-100 dashboard - three recessed `46 × 12` resource plates, three raised `40 × 29` command plates whose pressed state is derived in script, and a pop on only the slot that rose - plus the `72 × 54` minimap, the `208 × 14` wave chip, a transient safe-area toast, and the eight-card deck. It decides no outcome. Chip labels trim with an ellipsis, so every string they can show is measured against its label by `tactical_hud_validation`. `TacticalBuildSystem` owns the shared pool, host validation, structure commits, tower/trap runtime snapshots, placement overlap, blocking-structure collision, and the Base relocation transaction. `PlacedStructure2D` gives each catalog ID a distinct Blender silhouette; towers and utilities own `WorldStatic` footprints and the three ground traps never block. `GameAudioController` extends `AudioManager` with six bounded `AudioStreamPlayer2D` world voices and an eight-voice mono UI pool. `PlayerProjectile` renders a tracer and projectile head, ray-collides continuously against WorldStatic/Enemies, applies host-authorized damage, and always frees itself on impact or timeout.

**Feedback stack** (`src/feedback/`, `src/visual/`). `VfxDirector` pools muzzle flashes, impact bursts, and death debris. `CameraShake2D` turns trauma into a bounded `Camera2D.offset` and never writes the focus `position` that `PlayerAvatar` owns. The damage vignette is a uniform inside the single `weather_overlay.gdshader` pass. Camera shake, damage wash, weather, and pulse amplitude each carry an accessibility scale where zero means absent, not dim, and `GameWorld.apply_settings` sets all four from `GameSettings` (section 11). `EnemyAgent2D` banks a per-variant knockback impulse on a landed hit, integrates it through `move_and_slide()` in both the walking and attacking branch, suppresses it by the trap movement multiplier, and decays it to exact rest. None of these changes a health total, damage number, or authoritative position.

**One bar and one contact, shared rather than copied.**

- `HealthBar2D` is the only health readout (survivors, Base Core, horde, structures). Thin parts are authored in screen pixels through `_pixel()`, which divides the owner's own scale back out, so outline, bevel, and ticks are one screen pixel on every owner including the 2× Base Core; the bar body stays scaled. The Base bar is 64 world units, about seventy percent of the camp's alpha width, and `health_readability_validation` derives that bound from the shipped texture. It is the only owner with `hide_at_full = false`, so at rest `REST_DARKEN` darkens its fill - darkens, never fades, because alpha would let terrain tint it - and the bevel is lifted by `BEVEL_LIGHTEN` before that darkening, not after. `fill_color()` and `bevel_color()` are both public and the gate compares them as a ratio. Structures use a child bar at seventy percent of footprint width with two ticks and `hide_at_full = true`. The bar is a lit world-space node; the camp's `AmberLight` warms it.
- `GroundShadow` is the only contact language: `draw_ellipse`, `PROP_STRENGTH = 0.60`, depth capped by `MAX_CONTACT_DEPTH`. `ActorGroundShadow2D` sizes an actor's contact from its measured stance - survivors `base_radius = 16.0` world units at `Shadow.position = Vector2(0, 12.75)` (8.5 sheet pixels through the 1.5 scale), enemies through `EnemyPresentationDefinition.contact_radius` and `contact_offset` in sheet pixels. Never derive a contact from `marker_radius`, which describes a threat ring rather than a body.
- `GroundFissure` is the only crack language: a groove that meanders off its course, tapers to hairline tips, and carries a faint lit lip down-right of its dark core. That lip is what separates a groove from a stick, whose shadow falls the other way. Decor cracks, road transverse and longitudinal cracks, and the procedural-building fallback all draw through it. `GroundRubble` is the only loose-debris language: an irregular piece with a drop shadow, a darker broken side, and a lit top, used by the Ostari lot's berm and the decor rubble heaps. Both are static-only and listed in the export closure.
- Grep for the primitives a shared class replaced (`draw_rect` health bars, `draw_circle` contact discs, straight `draw_line` cracks) rather than trusting its claim to have replaced them. `PlacedStructure2D` hand-rolled both of the first two until that grep found it.

`ActorGroundShadow2D.GROUND_FLATTEN = 0.46` is a judged value, not a derivation. The bake camera's 52° elevation projects a ground circle to sin 52° = 0.788 and the map is drawn top-down at 1.0; 0.46 blends the two by eye. **Enemies.** `EnemyAgent2D`, `EnemyPresentationCatalog`, and `EnemyPresentation2D` keep eight stable simulation variant IDs and give each a distinct scene, tint family, silhouette treatment, marker language, and motion cadence. All eight read from one baked actor family: one camera, lighting rig, and palette bake eight world-space heading rows per clip, so a goliath reads as twice a walker in its pixels rather than through a multiplier. `ActorFacing` selects a row by dot product against measured headings, each baked scene pins its sprite to the projected origin the bake recorded, and `EnemyPresentationCatalog.ACTOR_SCALE = Vector2(1.0, 1.0)`, so a texel lands on a logical pixel and nearest filtering means what it says. Status presentation mirrors replicated slow/root state without per-enemy lights or particles. Read scales out of the source, not out of this document.

**Benchmark.** `visual_validation.tscn` is the integrated art-direction benchmark, not a shipping level: the three weathered surface families, all resource nodes, both characters, the Base Core, identity typography, and one weather pass in the exact logical viewport. Compare any shader, import, or palette change against it before accepting it.

## 8. Visual language

The world uses deep charcoal and petroleum green as its value base, with warm corrosion and cool patina creating large environmental zones. The Base Core is the only dominant Amber Gold source, allowing it to pull visual focus even when all other effects are active. White-metal and Cyan technology occupy smaller high-value islands so resource readability never competes with the refuge at equal scale.

| Semantic role | Color | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Void charcoal | `#060909` | Outlines, deepest cavities, silhouette separation |
| Worn steel | `#596360` | Machinery, plates, neutral structure |
| Rust bloom | `#BC431A` | Corrosion, danger history, damaged edges |
| Rotten timber | `#74401F` | Organic structure and salvage context |
| Copper patina | `#1C8270` | Weathered technology and world contrast |
| Base Core amber | `#FFB52E` | Safety, refuge, primary focal energy |
| Heikki gold | `#FFD45A` | Player 1 identity and utility mass |
| Shane cyan | `#31E6E6` | Player 2 identity and technical asymmetry |
| Wood signal | `#FFD700` | Resource-node semantic emission |
| Metal signal | `#E0E0E0` | Resource-node semantic emission |
| Tech signal | `#00FFFF` | Resource-node semantic emission |

Surface weathering is procedural and parameterized through a single CanvasItem shader with Rust, Decayed Wood, and Oxidized Copper modes. The implementation uses bounded hash/value-noise operations without dynamic loops, which keeps instruction cost predictable on mobile. Backgrounds may use strong weathering, while interactable silhouettes should reduce `weathering` or apply clean edge masks.

The Base Core aura uses an additive radial shader and a separate `PointLight2D`. Resource and character scenes use a four-sample outline rather than a large convolution kernel, trading subpixel perfection for stable mobile cost. Godot 4 performs CanvasItem light processing in the normal draw pass, and its official [CanvasItem shader reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/canvas_item_shader.html) and [2D lights guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html) define the behavior used here.

**Shader contract.** These hold for every CanvasItem shader under `shaders/`. The measurements that established each one are in [`docs/art-log/08-visual-language.md`](docs/art-log/08-visual-language.md).

- **`blend_mix`, never `unshaded`.** An unshaded CanvasItem receives neither `CanvasModulate` nor any `PointLight2D`, so it holds full daylight value while the world falls to the blue hour around it, and the Base Core's own `AmberLight` cannot light it. The additive `blend_add` Base Core aura is the only family allowed to hold night value.
- **Entry `COLOR` is already the textured, modulated fragment.** Write `vec4 source = COLOR;`. The old idiom `texture(TEXTURE, UV) * COLOR` multiplies the fetch by itself and ships every channel squared; seven shaders carried it, and each keeps the old line in a comment so it is not copied back.
- **Outlines are screen-space.** Offset taps by `fwidth(UV) * outline_width * stretch` with `stretch = (1.0 / SCREEN_PIXEL_SIZE.x) / 480.0` (`LOGICAL_VIEWPORT_WIDTH`), never by `TEXTURE_PIXEL_SIZE`, which delivers a different weight for every sprite scale. Shipped widths: the world sprite family and both survivor scenes `1.6`; the camp `2.2`, heavier because its near-`Void charcoal` contour must separate it from its own Amber light; resource identity rims `1.4`, the smallest width whose four-tap ring stays at least one logical pixel on a 45° edge.
- **World sprite grade.** `WorldObstacle2D._get_environment_finish` ships `sleek_sprite_finish` at contrast `1.30` (the knee - above it each step buys half as much separation, and the uniform range ends at 1.50), `shadow_tint 0.24`, and `shadow_lift 0.025`, which puts the crush floor at luma 12.74, clear of the 8.4 reserved for Void charcoal. `PlacedStructure2D` keeps the shader defaults on measurement: its structures are median-typical of the world and already separated from both grounds.
- **Textures are judged at the scale they minify.** Resource icon SVGs import with mipmaps and draw with `TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS`. Minification is a physical-pixel quantity under `canvas_items` stretch, so a 1× capture overstates texture aliasing (a worst-case bound), while a contour authored through `fwidth` is a logical quantity and exact at 1×.
- **Pulse is one global.** `bespren_pulse_amount` (`project.godot` `[shader_globals]`) scales the pulse in `toon_emissive_outline`, `volumetric_aura`, and `base_scale_glow`, and `CorePulseDriver.pulse_scale` scales the refuge light. At zero each pulse holds at its own mean, so switching it off stops motion and never changes brightness.
- **Runtime tint corrects hue, not value.** `WorldObstacle2D.WILD_TREE_TINT = Color(0.60, 1.0, 0.70)` folds into entry `COLOR` ahead of the grade and moves the straw-yellow firs into the foliage band; the ambient chunk multiplies the same constant. A baked value defect needs a rebake.

Every world light carries `range_z_min = -20` so it reaches the ground layer rather than stopping at the gameplay plane. The rendering matrix in section 16 puts terrain at `z_index = -20`, and a light whose Z range starts at the default −10 lights the actors while leaving the ground they stand on unlit - a pool of illuminated bodies floating over dark terrain. The value is set at exactly three sites: `scenes/characters/network_player.tscn:86`, `scenes/world/base_core.tscn:71`, and `src/tactical/placed_structure_2d.gd:406`.

The full-screen weather pass combines diagonal rain, drifting ash, a cold storm tint, and restrained vignette in one draw. It must remain a single overlay and may never intercept input; `WeatherOverlay` forces `MOUSE_FILTER_IGNORE`. Future weather states should change uniforms or swap bounded presets instead of stacking multiple screen-space materials.

## 9. Blender-to-Godot art pipeline

`tools/art/generate_character_sprites.py` creates a dedicated `BESPREN_SPRITE_STAGE` scene in Blender and leaves the user’s default scene intact. It builds both survivors from reproducible primitives, applies PBR materials and emission, adds three-point lighting plus Freestyle outlines, then renders 320×320 transparent PNGs into `assets/2d/characters/`. `tools/art/generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` uses the same isolated-scene policy to render the camp refuge and eight mechanically distinct structures into `assets/2d/structures/`, including the violet-triggered Razor Snare. Both scripts resolve the project root from their own location.

The render camera is orthographic and elevated at a top-down three-quarter angle. Heikki receives broader shoulders, rust tank mass, and a Gold chest slash; Shane receives a slimmer torso, asymmetrical pauldron, Cyan tech cell, and antenna. These differences deliberately survive desaturation and small-display viewing rather than relying on hue alone.

The animated actor family is baked through the same offline discipline. `tools/art/run_actor_bake.py` declares the roster as the game's actual cast — the two survivors and the eight `EnemyAgent2D` variants — and `build_actor_body.py` authors each body directly onto the CC0 KayKit skeleton so a goliath and a crawler differ in mass rather than in tint. `bake_animated_actor.py` renders every clip through one orthographic camera, lighting rig, and palette, laying frames out as `rows = 8 compass headings, columns = frames`; `pack_actor_sheets.py` downsamples them on the host interpreter and records each sheet's cell size, content bounds, per-row variation, and `ground_offset` — the projected world origin, derived from the camera framing rather than measured from alpha — into `sheets/sheet_manifest.json`. `tools/asset_pipeline/build_actor_sprite_frames.gd` then writes one `SpriteFrames` and one `AnimatedSprite2D` scene per actor, pinning each sprite to that recorded origin, and logs clips, animation counts, and foot offsets in `actor_frames_manifest.json`. Sheets import with VRAM compression and `process/fix_alpha_border`, so Android ships only the `.etc2.ctex` variant with the cutout edge intact. `ActorFacing` selects a row by dot product against the baked headings at runtime; no armature, mesh, or `Node3D` ships.

Runtime SVGs remain vector masters so they can be edited without lossy round trips, but Godot imports them as textures for scene use. New art must follow `type_subject_variant_state` naming, keep masters under `assets/2d/`, and be inspected at 1× logical size. A vector master delivered through a `Sprite2D` is also delivered through a texture filter, and section 8 records what the three resource icons cost when theirs was wrong: a 128-pixel raster read without mipmaps at a 5.7x minification aliases on every sub-pixel move, mipmaps fix it, and the 1× rule here is a worst-case bound for a texture rather than the shipped condition, because minification is a physical-pixel quantity under `canvas_items` stretch.

**Environment families.** Each is baked from a dedicated workshop behind `.gdignore` and packed into one atlas with a manifest recording sources, licenses, alpha bounds, regions, and hashes. At runtime they replace presentation only: collision, flow mask, stable seeds, obstacle ordering, and peer-one movement stay canonical.

Sprites use linear mipmaps and the shared forest/urban `sleek_sprite_finish` instances. Atomic Realm-derived images may ship inside the game but may never be redistributed as an asset pack. The urban ground's slab lattice is the `concrete_pavement_03` material's own mosaic, not a tiling repeat.

**Pipeline contract.** The record behind each rule - every rebake, sweep, and refuted instrument - is [`docs/art-log/09-art-pipeline.md`](docs/art-log/09-art-pipeline.md).

- **Judge a bake at the size it delivers** - its `scale` times the 0.38 gameplay zoom, at 1× logical - never at PNG resolution. A survivor delivers into about 33 pixels, a structure about 35 to 40, a resource node about 19. Verdicts taken at bake resolution have reversed at delivered size in both directions.
- **The bake camera basis is authored geometry.** Survivor bodies are yawed by the camera azimuth (`_face_camera`, 33.18°) so that width and bilateral symmetry stop trading against each other in projection. Heikki's rim light is a desaturated cool grey and Shane's is his Cyan (`RIM_COLOR_HEIKKI` / `RIM_COLOR_SHANE`), so hue separates Gold from Cyan instead of one shared rim wearing Shane's colour across both; accent emission is 0.4 and 0.5 because AgX burns off the hue of an over-driven accent.
- **AgX Medium High Contrast desaturates monotonically with brightness**, so removing a light reads as darker and more saturated at once. Compare a bake against the previous PNG, not against an expectation.
- **Determinism.** A rebake of unmodified source must reproduce the shipped pixels: exactly (max per-channel delta 0) for the camp, structure, and character families, where only the PNG `Date` and `RenderTime` chunks differ; within one unit on no more than about 0.1 percent of pixels for the EEVEE wild and salvage family. Anything larger is a regression to explain. A recorded `sha256` identifies the shipped file; `generator_sha256` pins the source.
- **Manifests.** Re-run `tools/art/write_generated_asset_manifest.py` after any camp, structure, or character rebake; it writes one manifest per family. The Poly Haven atlases carry packer-written manifests, and the gates pin the wild atlas PNG rather than its manifest, so a stale wild manifest is caught by nothing.
- **Atlas regions** are `Rect2` literals derived as `cell_region.xy + padded_atlas_region.xy` from the packer manifest; `padded_atlas_region` alone is cell-local and samples the wrong cell. Several tables are duplicated across files - `WILD_TREE_REGIONS` lives in both `world_obstacle_2d.gd` and `world_ambient_scenery_chunk_2d.gd` - so re-derive every copy by script after a pack, never one by eye.
- **Contact point is not the sprite pin.** The actor manifest's `ground_offset` (called `foot_offset` in `actor_frames_manifest.json`) is where the bake camera projected the world origin: the right pin for the pixels and the wrong point for the feet. Contact geometry is measured separately per actor (section 7).
- **Wild family.** Twelve pinned CC0 sources, 29 frames; the game draws only five tree yaws (three `fir_tree_01`, two `tree_small_02`). The fir's baked ground mound is cut by a holdout plane at `FIR_GROUND_CLIP = 0.40`, and the contact grade ships at `CONTACT_FLOOR 0.60` with a lateral footprint weight. `run_wild_bake.py` rebuilds the report from scratch, so a family bake is always both families. Every non-tree frame stays `forbidden_pending_human_visual_veto`: at 1× the root clusters read as tan boulders with no contact and the branches as black worms. The salvage atlas is a packed reserve with no runtime consumer.
- **Village fences** alternate their flip by construction - one seeded `fence_flip_phase` per fence, `flip_h = phase != (segment_index % 2 == 1)` - with bounded slip and size wobble from `_unit_noise`; collision, flow, and seeds are untouched. A per-segment coin flip cannot deliver adjacent variety.
- **Archive.** The first 2D catalog pipeline and its pixel-art output live under `tools/legacy/asset_catalog_2d/`, excluded by `.gdignore` since 2026-09-25; see its README before restoring anything.

**Open art items.** Fir canopy mass still reads as a stalk with tiers at 1× (candidates: a two-clone clump in the bake or a denser CC0 source); `GROUND_FLATTEN` needs a rendered sweep; the village houses are three-quarter bakes beside a top-down barricade, and the wider city set shares that projection mismatch; `WILD_TREE_TINT` should be dropped after a pine rebake.

## 10. Mobile rendering and performance policy

The project selects Godot’s `mobile` rendering method and Vulkan on Android, with OpenGL fallback enabled for unsupported devices. Godot’s Mobile renderer is designed around mobile GPU bandwidth and tile-based rendering, while HDR 2D would switch the viewport to higher-bandwidth RGBA16F; this foundation therefore leaves HDR 2D disabled. Emission is produced through controlled CanvasItem color and local lights rather than a screen-wide glow post-process.

The official [Godot 4.7 rendering architecture](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html) documents the Mobile renderer’s lower bandwidth path and the cost of extra full-screen passes. The [ProjectSettings reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_projectsettings.html) identifies `mobile` as the modern lower-overhead renderer for mobile devices. These sources justify the current renderer, single weather overlay, disabled HDR 2D, and lack of desktop post-processing.

| Budget | Foundation target | Escalation rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Logical resolution | 480×270 | Change only with UI, readability, and thermal evidence |
| Frame target | 60 FPS preferred, 30 FPS fallback | Measure on representative Android hardware |
| Frame time | 16.67 ms preferred | Quality tier may reduce weather/light energy at sustained misses |
| Full-screen custom passes | 1 | Combine atmosphere in the existing weather shader |
| Shadow-casting 2D lights | 0 in foundation | Add only after profiling and readability proof |
| Dynamic shader loops | 0 | Use bounded math or authored lookup textures |
| Local players | 2 | More seats require a new camera/input/UI contract |

The current showcase intentionally stresses several local lights at once for visual verification. Shipping scenes should use visibility culling, turn off presentation processing when off-screen, and limit overlapping lights on the same canvas region. Optimization must respond to profiler and device evidence, not to arbitrary code-size or asset-count targets.

## 11. Android UX and accessibility contract

The logical viewport is 480×270 in landscape with `canvas_items` stretch. `GameHUD` renders on CanvasLayer 100, converts Android safe-area insets into logical coordinates, and uses a `148 x 58` top-left dashboard. Its 8,584-pixel area is 50.9% smaller than the former `208 x 84` top-left panel/status union while `Make Base`, `Build`, and `Start Night` each retain an independent `44 x 44` touch target. Wood/Metal/Tech remain one compact row of three `46 x 12` icon plates. The count stays at font 8 because the geometry leaves no alternative: the `44 x 44` command row consumes 44 of the panel’s 58 pixels, a font 8 line is exactly 12 tall and font 9 is 13, and a 59-pixel panel would clear the area cap by four square pixels while falsifying the 50.9% figure above. Legibility therefore comes from the plate, the icon and the right alignment rather than from glyph size, and the spelled-out resource word survives on the pickup toast, which is where the mapping is taught. Wave state moved to a separate safe chip and status feedback is a transient nonblocking toast. The interactive minimap remains `72 x 54` at top-right. `TacticalBuildDeck` opens as a safe-area top sheet with Towers, Traps, and Utilities tabs, live resource chips, visual thumbnails, remembered selection per category, and at most three cards per page with overflow pagination. Physical-device ergonomics, cutout behavior, thermals, and lifecycle interruption still require representative Android hardware.

Identity must remain redundant across hue, silhouette, iconography, and placement.

**Accessibility settings.** The StartMenu's OPTIONS button (end of the session row, 44 × 44) opens an opaque sheet with one row per `GameSettings` scale: camera shake, damage flash, weather, and glow pulse. Every row has 44 × 44 step buttons, quarter steps, OFF at zero, and each step is saved to `user://settings.cfg` immediately, so a backgrounded app has no unsaved state to lose. A missing or malformed file falls back to full strength. `GameWorld` applies the stored values on entry, and `settings_lifecycle_validation` measures each effect at zero rather than reading its property back.

**Lifecycle.** On `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_PAUSED` or focus loss, every session releases held touches and zeroes movement; Solo also pauses the tree and resumes on return, and the world never leaves its own pause set when it exits. A LAN host does not pause, because a paused host would stall its client's snapshots; a client grace window is a protocol change and belongs to device certification. Controller disconnect and late Player 2 join remain required lifecycle cases for the later interaction milestone.

**Measurement rules.** Each of these was learned by getting a number wrong first; the episodes are in `docs/art-log/`.

- *Instruments.* A capture gate proves nothing about the build until it is re-run, and its noise floor is two runs of the same code. Pause the `SceneTree` before a two-pass difference capture and pin `TIME`-driven materials, because pausing does not stop shader `TIME`. A windowed run returns the physical window (960×540), not the logical viewport - derive scale from the image or render into a 480×270 `SubViewport`. A near-black capture background cannot test a dark shadow or a dark subject. Reuse the instrument that produced a reference, not a description of it. Check that an artifact is still produced before reading anything into it holding still.
- *Estimators.* A metric that includes a feature drawn outside the subject - an outline ring, a health bar, a light spill - is measuring that feature; mask on source alpha downsampled through the same filter as the colour. A constant answer across a varied population, or a 2.7× spread across a cast, is a broken estimator. When two estimators of one quantity disagree, find which assumption the subject violates rather than averaging. A mean far above its median means the mask caught a second population. A hue band that straddles two anchors reports one as the other. An absolute luma threshold cannot compare a day frame with a night one; cut by a fraction of the local ground. An optimum on every boundary of a grid is a broken search. A family mean over an 8× spread cannot place a second family below it. An autocorrelation with no peak at a candidate period is a negative result about tiling, whatever the eye reports.
- *Operators and gates.* A compressive operator applied after a treatment undoes it, and a gate that tests the input to a compressive operator does not test its output. A guard set at a contract anchor needs a value-independent companion. A floor derived at one parameter value is not the floor at another. A uniform that compensates a darker source can carry it past a criterion its raw values would fail - read the parameter. A runtime multiply can fix hue but not a baked value defect, and a silhouette defect that survives every grade is geometry, fixed in the bake. An offline model can be right about a shader that is wrong; after a shader fix, re-run the model's comparison before re-deriving anything. A per-segment coin flip does not deliver adjacent variety.
- *Records.* Read constants and scales out of the source, not out of this document, and check what a constant describes before matching another value to it. A docstring that states a derivation is a claim that can be checked arithmetically. A grep that omits a field cannot clear that field. Confirm that the shipped path draws an asset before measuring the asset. A bake measured at source resolution when it delivers at one eighth is measuring line work no player sees. A pocket table and an obstacle table authored separately will overlap somewhere; record the finding rather than moving the pocket. A 1× review is a measurement with a verdict, and a deferred review is a promotion by default.
- *World.* Layout frames do not show the player's view; judge the world at 0.38 on the tour. A vector primitive authored in world units ships in screen pixels - a 26-unit frame is a 10-pixel border at 0.38. Occupancy (pixels departing from their local mean) finds empty frames but counts noise as detail, so a fix can lower it; every occupancy figure needs a verdict taken off the frame. A density fix that adds collision is a navigation change, so add visual-only layers on their own seed streams and prove the flow hash held. A new dress layer can take a host pocket from a card gate; count it in that gate's envelopes. Test a relayout against both a translated and a mirrored copy of its sibling.

## 16. Tactical world and salvage slice

`BesprenWorldMap2D` defines a centered 14×14 macro-grid with 2,048-unit cells and exact ±14,336 playable extents. Kaupunki occupies the northwest blocks, Ostari forms a collapsed commercial choke in the north-east interior, two separate Kylät occupy the south, and Metsä/Luonto wraps the perimeter and wilderness belts. The asphalt cross, district branches, dirt village paths, the camp spur, and the forest perimeter route form one connected visual road graph.

**Road hierarchy.** Each route carries a `WorldCompositionContract.RoadGrade`, and the three grades read apart at 1× through width, edge, and negative space: the asphalt spine (420-unit bed, 160 screen pixels at 0.38), dirt branches (360, 137 pixels), and the wilderness perimeter track (230, 88 pixels), which has a soft verge and a grass crown between two ruts and no paint. The perimeter keeps the dirt grade's 480-unit corridor for every clearance rule, so narrowing it moved nothing seeded. `WorldRoadNetwork2D` draws every route's shoulder before any bed, as two passes of chunks, so a junction reads as one surface, and paint lines and dashes stop inside it. A free route end tapers its shoulder onto the bed over 300 units and finishes in a ragged cap that fades toward its tip. The spur (route 9, `camp_spur`, dirt) runs from the camp's open ring, 465 units from the refuge, to the east-west spine. It is the one road allowed inside the camp's 1,600-unit seclusion ring: seclusion rules measure with `get_minimum_road_edge_distance_excluding_camp_spur()`, while the dress layers keep the full distance so the spur stays a clear path through the trees.

The rendering matrix is absolute: ground `TileMapLayer`s, roads and the camp clearing use `z_index = -20` (the clearing is ordered after the terrain overlay and before `TerrainDetails`, so the spur visibly leaves it), non-colliding rubble, moss and the grave rows use `-5`, the forest understory uses `-4`, and gameplay/static occlusion participants use `5` beneath explicit Y-sort containers. Every generated building shell, mall wall, wreck, house, fence, tree trunk, rock, and world edge owns a `CollisionShape2D` on `WorldStatic`. The same obstacle objects bake a 112×112 occupancy mask and resolve peer-one movement before authoritative positions are broadcast.

`BesprenResourceScatter2D` produces 87 stable resource IDs from a fixed seed: three Base Core teaching anchors and 84 stratified global sectors. Spawn rejection uses playable bounds, canonical obstacle footprints, clearance, and minimum spacing. Each stable ID derives a deterministic requirement of three, four, or five accepted interactions. Clients submit only the existing `interact` command; peer one uses its stored authoritative player position and per-peer focus lock, advances one reliable ordered revision, grants zero on intermediate steps, and grants the same single unit only when the node completes. Late-join snapshots include availability and partial progress for every node. See `docs/WORLD_MAP_FOUNDATION.md` for the coordinate, layering, network, and validation contracts.

`is_position_walkable` is answered through a 512-unit obstacle broadphase (`OBSTACLE_BROADPHASE_CELL`) rather than a linear scan, because `resolve_player_motion` calls it up to three times per player per 20 Hz tick. A query pads by `clearance * sqrt(2)`, since a rotated rectangle expands along its own axes, so the grid stays a strict superset of the exact test; over 6,000 queries weighted toward obstacle edges it runs 55× faster than the scan with zero mismatches against a brute-force reference.

The ground-cover family is the ecology layer beneath the obstacles: 3,245 clusters and 38,947 elements at two draw commands per cluster across 170 render chunks, gated by biome, walkability, road verge, and the camp clearance. It draws with no material, which is not the `unshaded` opt-out - it darkens with the night `CanvasModulate` exactly as the ground does - and its density constants stand on measurement rather than on the stale "five elements per screen" complaint. The record for both paragraphs is [`docs/art-log/16-world.md`](docs/art-log/16-world.md).

**Visual-only dress at the player's zoom.** Metsä's colliding stands are three or four trees per 2,048-unit cell, and adding more would close flow-field routes (each trunk is rasterised with 215 units of clearance). So the forest's density comes from `WorldForestUnderstory2D`: saplings, shrubs (the two dense firs cropped above the bole, so no unreviewed wild frame ships), ferns, stumps, moss rocks, and deadfall, about 2,570 elements with one hashed candidate per 230-unit cell. It leaves an empty bowl inside 1,180 units of the camp, raises a wall out to 2,150, scales its fill by the biome mask's forest share, thins to a sparse scatter in far wilderness, and leaves the composition's wilderness pockets to the layers that dress them. `WorldCampClearing2D` lays the bowl's trodden earth and a worn path to each satellite in the dirt roads' lit grain. East Kylät is its own place rather than West translated: a three-house hamlet west of its road, a caretaker's house, a broken pole line, and a walled grave plot east of the road. `WorldGraveyard2D` draws headstone and cross rows inside that plot. The plot's six wall runs each stop 150 units short of the next, so every corner and mid-side is open to a survivor. All three layers are configured after the canonical obstacle and flow pipeline, on seed streams of their own, and own no physics, flow, resource, or gameplay state; `world_map_validation` pins the flow-mask hash to prove it.

This keeps the current foundation reproducible while respecting the project’s source material.
