Future Base mechanics must compose beside this view rather than turning the visual driver into a game-state authority.

The feedback stack under `src/feedback/` and `src/visual/` reports the simulation without ever deciding it. `VfxDirector` owns pooled muzzle flashes, impact bursts, and death debris; `CameraShake2D` converts trauma into a bounded `Camera2D.offset` and never writes the focus `position` that `PlayerAvatar` owns; `ActorGroundShadow2D` grounds every actor, and it finally does it on one anchor - both families now take `base_radius` from a stance measured off the baked sheets, the enemies through `EnemyPresentationDefinition.contact_radius` and the survivors through a value in `network_player.tscn`, and the paragraphs below are about the two separate sittings that took. Where that ellipse is centred was wrong on the same class for the same kind of reason, and section 9 carries it, because the number it was centred on comes out of the bake rather than out of this stack; and the damage vignette is a uniform inside the single existing `weather_overlay.gdshader` pass rather than a second full-screen pass. `HealthBar2D` is one drawn node shared by the survivors, the Base Core, and the horde, so one outline weight, chip drain, and critical cadence read as one authored language. A bar also owes something to the thing it describes, and both halves of that were learned the hard way. Size: the Base bar was authored at 108 world units under a camp that measures 92 across its own alpha, so the refuge's readout drew at 117 percent of the refuge with 24 units of bare ground between them - a widget lying on the terrain rather than the Base's own state. It is 64 now, seventy percent of the camp and still wider than a survivor's 37, and `health_readability_validation.gd` derives that bound from the shipped texture's alpha bounds through the `Core` node's scale so a rebake moves it rather than leaving it stale. Loudness: the Base is the only owner that passes `hide_at_full = false`, so its bar is on screen for the whole match, and measured off the camp capture it ran at mean luma 202 and 0.84 saturation in a frame whose mean is 57 and whose refuge sits at 71 - the brightest sustained block in the shot, permanently, holding the pull CLAUDE.md 8 reserves for the Base Core. `REST_DARKEN` drops the fill while the bar has nothing to say and the first point of damage restores it in full, so a hit now wakes the readout instead of merely shortening something that was already shouting. It darkens rather than fades, because alpha would let the terrain behind it tint the bar, which is the property the outline exists to prevent. That treatment reached the fill and not the bevel, and the two paragraphs after this one are the sitting that took. Weight: the thin parts are authored in screen pixels and divided into world units through one constant, and that conversion assumed every owner sat at scale one - but `game_world.tscn` instances the Base Core at `Vector2(2, 2)`, so the refuge drew every outline, bevel and tick at two screen pixels against the survivors' one, which is precisely the shared weight the class exists to guarantee. `_pixel()` divides the owner's own scale back out; the bar body is deliberately left scaled, because the Base being physically larger than a survivor is the design. The gate now reads that instance scale out of `game_world.tscn` too, so its screen-pixel bounds are evaluated against the size the refuge bar actually ships at rather than half of it. The bar is a world-space `Node2D` and is therefore lit: the camp's Amber Gold `PointLight2D` warms it and its pulse gives the readout the refuge's own cadence, and measured off the captures it holds 2.4x the surrounding terrain luma by day and 2.9x at night, so being lit widens its contrast rather than costing it. `EnemyAgent2D` adds the weight half of that language: a landed hit banks a per-variant knockback impulse that is integrated through `move_and_slide()` in both the walking and the attacking branch, suppressed by the same movement multiplier a trap applies, and decayed to exact rest. Every effect here obeys CLAUDE.md 11 - camera shake, damage wash, and pulse amplitude each carry an accessibility scale where zero means absent rather than dim - and none of them changes a health total, a damage number, or an authoritative position. `GroundShadow` is the parallel contact language, and both it and `HealthBar2D` have now taught the same lesson: a class that documents itself as the one implementation does not thereby become it. `PlacedStructure2D` hand-rolled both. Its bar was a `draw_rect` pair in a hardcoded `#72b49a` at three units tall, with no outline, chip drain, threat band, critical cadence or accessibility scale, and it was drawn unconditionally - so every untouched tower carried a permanent bright mint block, measured at 1.55x to 4.33x the mean luma of its own structure and worst on Electric at 150.2 against a body at 34.7. That is precisely the defect this section already recorded for the Base bar, committed a second time in a second file. Its contact was a single `draw_circle` of near-black at a flat 0.42 alpha across 84 percent of the footprint, which removed 33 percent of the ground's value behind a hard edge and made a tower read as an object sitting in a hole - the "flat disc at any single opacity" that `GroundShadow`'s own docstring names as the thing it exists to retire, and a fourth divergent copy it had missed. Both now call the shared implementations: a `HealthBar2D` child at seventy percent of footprint width with two ticks and `hide_at_full = true`, and `GroundShadow.draw_ellipse` at `PROP_STRENGTH` with depth capped by `MAX_CONTACT_DEPTH`. Re-measured off `structure_visual_gameplay_scale.png` the bar's 76-pixel mint run is gone outright and a full-health tower now shows nothing, while the contact's flat floor at luma 11.9 against a 22.5 ground became a graded ramp bottoming at 17.6 - a 47 percent cut replaced by a 22 percent one that recovers to ground rather than ending at an edge. The standing instruction is to grep for the primitives a shared class replaced rather than trusting its claim to have replaced them.

That rest treatment reached the fill and left the loudest element of the readout untouched, and the mechanism is worth more than the fix. The bevel is one lifted row along the top of the fill - at three screen pixels tall a gradient is invisible, so a single bright band is the whole thing that stops the bar reading as flat vector art - and it was drawn as `fill.lightened(0.42)`. `Color.lightened` is affine toward white rather than a multiplier: it maps 0 to 0.42 and 1 to 1, so it is compressive, and applying it after a darkening re-inflates exactly what the darkening removed. `REST_DARKEN`'s 0.45 multiplier on the fill therefore arrived as 0.72 on the bevel. At rest the band sat at 1.844x its own fill authored and 1.888x delivered, against the 1.149x every other state the bar can be in ships; measured on the camp capture it ran at mean luma 210.7 in a frame whose mean is 52.0, and it held 182 of the 1,113 frame pixels above that value - 16.4 percent of the frame's brightest population, on a widget occupying 0.33 percent of the frame. **A compressive operator applied after a treatment undoes the treatment.** The fix is an order reversal in the resting branch alone - the band is lifted first and the result darkened, so the lift survives as a ratio instead of being re-inflated by it - plus the promotion of the magic 0.42 to `BEVEL_LIGHTEN` beside its sibling `CHIP_LIGHTEN`. Every other state routes through `fill_color().lightened(BEVEL_LIGHTEN)` and is byte-identical to before, and the chip needs no change at all, because a chip only exists while `_chip > _ratio` and that is never a resting state.

Why it survived a gate is the transferable half. `health_readability_validation.gd` asserted on `BEVEL_SCREEN_PX`, which is the bevel's *width*, and drove its whole rest treatment through `refuge.fill_color()` - so it tested the input to the compressive operator and never its output. **A gate that tests the input to a compressive operator does not test its output.** `bevel_color()` is public now for the same reason `fill_color()` is, and four checks compare the two lifts as a ratio rather than either as a value, taking the gate from `HEALTH READABILITY OK (63)` to `(67)`. Re-rendered through the same probe on the same ground, the bevel falls 210.7 to 130.7 while the fill is byte-identical at 110.0, the ground holds at 50.8 and the frame mean at 52.0 - so nothing else in the scene moved - and the widget's p90 falls 216.2 to 133.5 with its maximum 229.4 to 142.1, which is the practical statement of it: the readout no longer contains a pixel above the brightest resource node in the shot. The resting ratio is 1.169 delivered against 1.149 authored, in family with the alerted 1.107 and 1.149. That also settles `REST_DARKEN` itself on delivered pixels, which this section had only ever asserted: the bar that ran at mean luma 202 and 0.84 saturation now measures 86.2 and 0.46, all three resource nodes of comparable delivered area match or outrank it, and the camp's own fire core peaks at 251.4 against the widget's 142.1. Two method notes came out of it and both cost a wrong number first. The first fill measurement came back at mean 133.0 against a median of 117.5, because a green-dominance mask admits a warm bevel - **a mean fifteen luma above its own median is the signature of a mask that has caught a second population**, and a row profile is what separated them. And the docstring's "the fill lands near 85" is not wrong: 84.8 is the correct *authored* luma and 110.0 the delivered one, because the bar is a lit world-space node and the camp's `AmberLight` multiplies it by roughly (1.76, 1.27, 0.98). That shared class grounded two families that were never on one scale, and the survivors were the ones paying first. `EnemyPresentation2D` set `base_radius` to `marker_radius * SHADOW_FOOTPRINT_SCALE`, so an enemy's contact was derived from something and looked settled; `network_player.tscn` set it to a flat 9.5, so a survivor's was derived from nothing that outlives the sitting it was authored in. The enemy record further down this section is about what the principled-looking half cost. Rendered at the 0.38 gameplay scale that literal put 6 screen pixels of contact under an 11-pixel stance - 55 percent, with about 2.5 pixels of boot overhanging unmodified ground on each side, identical by day and by night. That is CLAUDE.md 9's own already-recorded 54 percent of his boot band, arrived at from the opposite direction and confirmed in shipped pixels. Depth was never the defect: the contact cut 26 percent of the ground's value, in family with the 22 percent the structure contact settles at. Width was.

The replacement is 16.0, and it is worth recording how it was picked rather than only what it is. Two independent denominators were measured off the baked sheets - Heikki's lower-body width of 33.8 world units against Shane's 30.6, and their bottom-decile foot widths of 19.0 and 17.2 - and 16.0 is the one radius where the contact just covers the stance on both (diameter over lower-body width 0.95 and 1.05) while landing inside the 1.5x-to-1.8x band of foot width (1.68 and 1.86). Two denominators that share no pixels agreeing on one number is the signal; a single ratio would not have been. The enemy median of roughly 23.8 was rejected outright, and that rejection is the transferable part: `marker_radius` describes the ring an enemy's threat is announced through, not the width of anything it stands on, so matching a survivor's footprint to it would have been matching a body to a UI affordance. **Check what a constant describes before matching another value to it.** A five-radius sweep at 9.5, 12.0, 14.5, 16.5 and 19.0 rendered through the real Mobile/Vulkan path bracketed the derivation rather than confirming it by assertion: 9.5 and 12.0 leave the outer boots standing on unshaded ground, 14.5 just reaches them, 16.5 covers the stance without overhang, and 19.0 detaches into a pool visibly wider than the body. Re-measured on the shipped review capture the contact is now 13 pixels under a 13-pixel stance at unchanged depth, 26 percent by day and 29 percent at night.

The sweep scene and its captures were deleted the moment they had answered, because CLAUDE.md 8 already records what a capture outliving the gate that writes it does to a later audit. Two method notes survive it. A band chosen to summarise a silhouette is itself the measurement: the bottom-decile sliver and the bottom-quartile band disagreed by a factor of two about the same shadow, and the sliver produced a confident 1.00x boot-width finding that the rendered frame reversed - name the band and check it against the frame before quoting a ratio. And an absolute luma threshold cannot compare a day frame to a night one: a fixed 2.0-luma cut asks for 7.4 percent of a 27.1 ground but 11.6 percent of a 17.2 one, so it clipped the shallow rim at night and made one geometry read as 13 pixels by day and 6 at night. Cut by a fraction of the local ground and the two frames agree to the pixel, which is what they were always doing.

The enemy half of that constant took its own sitting, and it is the more instructive one because the derived value looked principled. `SHADOW_FOOTPRINT_SCALE` multiplied `marker_radius`, so every variant's contact tracked the ring its threat is announced through - bars, prongs, an arc, runes, orbits, twins, a crown - and that ring is authored per marker language with no reason to track a stance. Measured off the baked sheets over idle and walk, all eight headings and every frame, the ratio of shipped contact diameter to lower-body width ran from 1.00 on the goliath to 2.69 on the crawler pack: the goliath stood wider than its own shadow while a crawler wore one 2.7x its whole lower body, drawn once per copy and therefore three times over. **A metric whose spread across a cast is 2.7x is measuring something other than what it claims to.** The constant is deleted rather than retuned, because no single multiple of a readability affordance can be right for eight bodies.

The lower-body estimator alone gives 22.32, so 22.5 ships, and it is also the only variant whose radius barely moves. **When two estimators of one quantity disagree, decide which of their assumptions the subject violates; do not average across the disagreement.**

The change was gated and then confirmed on delivered pixels, because this section already records that the survivors' radius was accepted only after a rendered sweep bracketed the derivation rather than an assertion confirming it. `SMOKE OK (207)`, `ENEMY PRESENTATION OK (44)`, `ENEMY IMPACT OK (42)`, `VFX DIRECTOR OK (133)` and `HEALTH READABILITY OK (67)` pass, and the Mobile/Vulkan enemy render gate re-ran clean at `variants=8 | captures=3`. That gate could not judge the change, though, for an arithmetic reason rather than an artistic one: its bands are authored at luma 17.2, and a contact at `GroundShadow`'s stacked 0.414 alpha and luma 9.1 can only take 3.3 luma out of a 17.0 ground, so before and after peaked at 2.8 and the ceiling was the stimulus. A near-black capture background cannot test a dark shadow. A temporary probe put the eight variants on the shipped day ground's own 22.4 instead, paused the tree, and wrote each frame twice with every shadow branch hidden on the second pass, so the contact is isolated by difference with no threshold involved at all. Two things that probe could not do are worth stating, because one of them nearly produced a number. Its body denominator is broken: it masks the shadow-free pass against a flat ground colour, so the marker ring, the health bar and the label all read as body and four different actors come back at an identical 72.0, which is the crop bound rather than a silhouette. That column was never quoted and must not be. Nor may the stance figures in the table above be cross-quoted against this section's recorded survivor widths: a band chosen to summarise a silhouette is itself the measurement, and the bottom-quartile band used here reads Heikki at 29.42 world units where the recorded figure is 33.8, and his foot band at 17.49 where the recorded figure is 19.0. The table is internally consistent because every row in it, survivors included, was measured in one band on the sheets. The probe, its scene and both captures are retired the moment they answered, on the same rule that retired the survivors' sweep. Two capture facts came out of it and both cost a wrong reading first. **A windowed Godot run returns the physical window size rather than the logical viewport** - this one arrived at 960 by 540, and every coordinate taken from it was wrong by exactly two until the scale was derived from the image instead of assumed. And **a two-pass difference capture has to pause the SceneTree first**, because an `AnimatedSprite2D` that advances one frame between the passes puts whole bodies into the difference: peak deltas of 84 and 102 and shadow widths of 50 before the freeze, 6.0 after.

One finding from the same work is recorded open rather than fixed. `ActorGroundShadow2D.GROUND_FLATTEN` is 0.46 and its docstring says that is what a ground circle projects to under the bake camera's 52 degrees. Working the actual rig basis - `aaa_bake_rig.py` builds a forward vector from elevation 52 and azimuth -45 at distance 24, then tracks it - a unit ground circle projects to 1.0 horizontal by sin(52 degrees) = 0.788 vertical, and 0.46 is sin(27.4 degrees). The docstring is arithmetically false whichever value ultimately ships. It is not corrected to 0.788 here, for three reasons: the world is drawn 1:1 top-down, which argues the other way toward 1.0, so the shipping number is a judged blend of the sprite's implied projection and the map's rather than a derivation from either; the prop families share no flatten with it at all, at 0.34 on atlas contacts, 0.55 on foliage and aspect-derived on structures; and this section's own precedent requires a rendered sweep before a shadow dimension changes, while the survivors' accepted look was settled at 0.46. **A docstring that states a derivation is a claim that can be checked arithmetically**, and this one was false in the same file whose sibling constant had just been refuted for the same species of reason - a number that describes one thing being used as though it described another.

`EnemyAgent2D`, `EnemyPresentationCatalog`, and `EnemyPresentation2D` keep the eight stable simulation variant IDs while assigning one distinct scene, scale, tint family, silhouette treatment, marker language, and motion cadence to each type. All eight now read from one baked actor family under `assets/2d/actors/`: a single camera, lighting rig and palette bake each variant into eight world-space heading rows per clip, so a goliath reads as twice a walker because of its pixels rather than a per-variant scale multiplier. `ActorFacing` picks the row by dot product against measured headings, every baked scene pins its sprite to the projected world origin the bake recorded, and the catalog therefore applies one uniform `Vector2(1.0, 1.0)` scale. The two quantities it does carry per variant both belong to the contact shadow rather than to the sprite: `contact_offset` moves that ellipse down, because the recorded origin is the right pin for the pixels and the wrong one for the feet, which section 9 measures; and `contact_radius` sizes it off the silhouette, which the paragraphs above measure. That constant read `Vector2(2.0, 2.0)` while the bake framed every clip on the union of all of them, so a death pose lying flat set the cell and a walker's idle resolved from about twenty real pixels which the scale then doubled. Doubling the bake density and halving the scale leaves every actor the same size on screen with real pixels behind it, and at exactly 1.0 a texel lands on a logical pixel, so the nearest filtering these sheets ask for finally means what it says. The survivors are the one exception to that uniformity and were halved by the same change: `PlayerAvatar` ships its baked actor at `BAKED_ACTOR_VISUAL_SCALE = 1.5`. Both numbers are recorded here because this paragraph carried the old one for long enough to corrupt a measurement taken against it - a cast-wide comparison of contact-shadow footprints read every enemy body at twice its shipped width, halved every enemy ratio, and produced a conclusion that reversed once the constant was read out of the code instead. **Read a scale out of the source, not out of this document; a stale constant in a contract does not announce itself, it silently corrupts whatever is measured against it.** Status presentation mirrors replicated slow/root state without adding a per-enemy light or particle budget.

`visual_validation.tscn` is the integrated art-direction benchmark and not a shipping level. It renders the three weathered surface families, all resource nodes, both characters, the Base Core, identity typography, and one full-screen weather pass in the exact logical viewport. Any later shader, import, or palette change should be compared against this scene before being accepted.
