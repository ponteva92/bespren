# Art log: Visual language

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md section 8 on 2026-09-25. This is the measurement record behind the contract: how each shipped value was found, what was refuted, and which instruments lied first. CLAUDE.md keeps the current contract and the rules learned; this file keeps the evidence. Numbers here are as recorded at the time and are not refreshed - read live values out of the source.

The world uses deep charcoal and petroleum green as its value base, with warm corrosion and cool patina creating large environmental zones. The Base Core is the only dominant Amber Gold source, allowing it to pull visual focus even when all other effects are active. White-metal and Cyan technology occupy smaller high-value islands so resource readability never competes with the refuge at equal scale.

Raising the world's contrast put that first claim at risk, so it was re-measured rather than assumed, and the check is recorded because it failed in an instructive direction. The complaint was that the forest canopy now reads as a warm gold mass competing with the refuge. Two estimators say it does not. Warm pixels - hue 30 to 70 degrees at saturation 0.35 or above - holding more than twice the frame mean luma are 0.00 percent of the forest density capture against 0.59 percent of the refuge frame, so there is no bright warm population outside the camp at all. And splitting that frame's 13.48 percent warm share by column band finds the mass somewhere else entirely: the dirt road is 7.87 percent of the frame and 58.4 percent of the whole warm population at mean luma 61.7 and hue 32.5 degrees, the treed half is 3.27 percent of frame and 24.2 percent of warm at a much darker 44.3 and a much greener 56.3 degrees, and the right verge is 2.29 and 17.0 percent at 45.9 and 40.3. The largest and brightest warm thing in a forest frame is the road, which is terrain drawn by `terrain_material_blend.gdshader` and was never touched by the obstacle grade. This is the third time a hue band drawn across two anchor families has reported one of them as the other, after the rust tank counted as Heikki's gold and the tent facet counted as Shane's cyan, and it is the first time the misattributed family was terrain rather than a prop. The hue half of the canopy complaint is closed; the separate objection to the trees' silhouette is not, and is not answered by any of this.

Surface weathering is procedural and parameterized through a single CanvasItem shader with Rust, Decayed Wood, and Oxidized Copper modes. The implementation uses bounded hash/value-noise operations without dynamic loops, which keeps instruction cost predictable on mobile. Backgrounds may use strong weathering, while interactable silhouettes should reduce `weathering` or apply clean edge masks.

The Base Core aura uses an additive radial shader and a separate `PointLight2D`. Resource and character scenes use a four-sample outline rather than a large convolution kernel, trading subpixel perfection for stable mobile cost. Godot 4 performs CanvasItem light processing in the normal draw pass, and its official [CanvasItem shader reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/canvas_item_shader.html) and [2D lights guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html) define the behavior used here.

`toon_emissive_outline.gdshader` is deliberately `blend_mix` and not `unshaded`, and the distinction is not cosmetic: an unshaded CanvasItem receives neither `CanvasModulate` nor any `PointLight2D`. Under the previous `unshaded` mode all six subjects wearing this material - the refuge, the three resource nodes, and both survivors - were exempt from the `night_atmosphere_2d.gd` transition, so the world fell to the blue hour around them while they held full daylight value; the camp read as a lit decal pasted onto a dark map and the resource nodes stayed fully saturated after dusk. It also meant the Base Core's own `AmberLight` lit the ground beneath the camp but never the camp itself, which is why the refuge could previously only be brightened by stacking flat additive terms. `terrain_material_validation.gd` already asserts this same responsiveness for the ground, and the things standing on that ground now obey the same rule. Confirmed on both survivors and all three resource nodes.

That fix was applied to one shader and left the same defect standing in two others, which is worth recording because the reason it survived was a bad audit rather than a hard problem. `sleek_canvas_grade.gdshader` and `sleek_sprite_finish.gdshader` were both `unshaded`, and between them they dress the ambient scenery, background decor and wilderness accent chunk families, every `WorldObstacle2D` - rock, stump, trunk, shell, house, fence, wreck and district fitting - and every `PlacedStructure2D` the player builds. So the entire built world and most of its dressing held full daylight value while the ground and the survivors fell to the blue hour. The measure is the share of pixels whose night-to-day luma ratio exceeds 0.92 in a world whose median ratio is 0.63, over the five day/night capture pairs the world gate actually writes: west village 10.00 percent, city barrier 7.46, forest camp composition 6.71, forest camp 5.28, open wilderness 0.27. After both shaders were switched to `blend_mix` the city barrier and the west village hold exactly zero exempt pixels and the remaining three hold 161, 67 and 8 - all of them day rgb (85, 67, 2) and (118, 95, 21), which is the refuge's own `blend_add` aura and is supposed to hold. Daylight paid nothing: mean frame luma matches the previous capture to two decimals in all five, because with no `CanvasModulate` in force the light pass composites the base draw unchanged. Neither shader ever needed `unshaded`; both are grading passes that write `COLOR` and perform no lighting of their own.

Two method notes came out of that, and both cost a wrong conclusion first. An exemption is invisible in a capture that does not contain the subject: the first reading used the open-wilderness frame, returned 0.27 percent, and would have closed the case. And the frame that finally isolated the second shader did it by not changing at all - the city barrier capture came back byte-identical after the first fix, which proves the frame holds no pixel of that material and its exempt blob was always something else. Against that, `world_forest_props` sat in the table through two rounds at a fixed 6.56 percent before its file date gave it away: it is an August artifact from a retired gate that the current twenty-five-capture set does not write, so it was never evidence about this build. Check that an artifact is still produced before reading anything into it holding still.

Those two shaders carried a second defect of the same species, and it is the more instructive one because nothing about it looks wrong in the source. `sleek_sprite_finish.gdshader` stepped its four outline taps by `TEXTURE_PIXEL_SIZE * outline_width`, which is an offset of one source texel in UV. What reaches the screen is therefore `outline_width` multiplied by the sprite's own scale and by the camera zoom, so one authored 1.6 delivers a different contour weight to every subject wearing the material. Measured across the twenty-three shipped subjects at the gameplay zoom of 0.38, the delivered thickness runs from 0.13 screen pixels to 2.83 - a 21.6x range on a constant - and it is thinnest on precisely the things the player builds:

An outline is a screen-space quantity - it is how heavy a contour looks to a player - so authoring it in texels means authoring it in a unit the player never sees. The offset is now `fwidth(UV) * outline_width * stretch`. `fwidth(UV)` is how far UV moves across one screen pixel, so the tap lands one screen pixel out whatever the subject's scale, for one derivative instruction and no new materials. Raw `fwidth` alone would have been wrong in a way that is easy to miss: it yields *physical* pixels, so under `canvas_items` stretch the same line would draw at a quarter weight on a 1080p panel and the offline simulation, which models logical pixels, would stop matching the render. `stretch` is `(1.0 / SCREEN_PIXEL_SIZE.x) / 480.0`, and `SCREEN_PIXEL_SIZE` is available to a canvas_item fragment shader without a screen texture being read, so section 10's single-full-screen-pass budget is untouched. The alternative was a material per quantised scale band, and it was rejected for being N things to keep correct rather than one: it re-breaks the moment any scale moves, and it cannot express a subject whose scale is chosen at runtime, which `PlacedStructure2D` does three different ways.

The fix was then confirmed on delivered pixels rather than on the algebra, because the whole point of the defect is that the authored number and the delivered number are different things. Horizontal dark-run lengths in the freshly captured structure frame give a short-run mean of 1.75 to 2.23 across the eight structures against the world props' 1.40 to 1.71 - one contour family, where before the structures carried 0.14 to 0.19 screen pixels against the world's 0.9 to 2.83. The slight excess is population rather than weight: a structure delivers into about 35 pixels, so its compressed interior line work merges with the ring, while a prop resolving into 127 to 578 pixels separates them. This is the same class as `HealthBar2D._pixel()` in section 7 - a screen-space quantity authored in a space that scales - and it is now the third, fourth and fifth instance of it. The fifth is `toon_emissive_outline.gdshader`, worn by the refuge at an effective 0.84 scale, the three resource nodes at 0.46, and both survivor scenes at 0.27, and the paragraphs below close it.

That fifth instance is now closed, and the thing that had held it back was retired by measuring it rather than by solving it. The objection on record was that this shader multiplies its width by an animated pulse and therefore could not take the same fix. The multiplier is `0.9 + pulse * 0.1` over a pulse that runs 0.52 to 1.00, which is a width factor of 0.952 to 1.000 - a five percent wobble no player can see, and not a reason for anything. What actually pulses on that material is the emission and the outline's alpha, and the fix touches neither. The offset is now `fwidth(UV) * animated_outline_width * stretch` against the same `LOGICAL_VIEWPORT_WIDTH = 480.0`, so the two shaders' widths finally mean the same unit.

The ratio here was mild beside the sprite shader's 21.6x - 4.39x across the wearers and 1.99x among the three that ship in-world - and the ratio was never the finding. The absolute values were. At the 0.38 gameplay zoom the refuge drew 0.766 screen pixels of contour, a resource node 0.385, and the legacy survivor scenes 0.174. A four-tap cross below one pixel is not a thin contour but very nearly no contour, because the taps land inside the sprite's own alpha and `neighbor_alpha - source.a` falls to zero along most of the boundary. Section 1 calls the Base Core the emotional centre of the game and it was the object drawing three quarters of a pixel of edge.

Every one of them was re-derived by reading `Texture2D.get_image().get_used_rect()` on the shipped texture and walking the scene's own scale chain, which is the same instruction section 7 already states about scales - read it out of the source rather than out of this document. The strongest finding of that audit is semantic rather than geometric. The three resource scenes set `outline_color` to their own locked anchor - Wood `Color(1, 0.843, 0, 1)`, Metal `Color(0.878, 0.878, 0.878, 1)`, Tech `Color(0, 1, 1, 1)` - and set `glow_color` to the same anchor beside it. What this material draws on a node is therefore not a contour at all, it is the identity rim section 7 counts as one of the channels a resource is recognised by, and at 0.385 delivered pixels that channel was not rendering. Section 11 requires identity to stay redundant across hue, silhouette, iconography and placement, and on a 19-pixel body the iconography is subpixel, so the rim was carrying load it had no width to carry. A hypothesis that all three merely inherited the shader's gold `glow_color` default was refuted by reading the full material blocks; the grep that raised it had not included the field. **A grep that omits a field cannot clear that field.**

Six scenes wear this material rather than the five an earlier pass listed - `shane.tscn` was missing from it, and enumerating them with `grep -rn` over `scenes src tests tools` instead of from the record is what found him. The six widths were then retuned on three separate arguments rather than one taste. The survivors take 1.6, which is the world sprite family's authored value verbatim: same shader idiom and, after the fix, the same unit, so the two families are one line weight by construction rather than by coincidence. The camp takes 2.2, deliberately 1.375x the world line, because it is the one wearer whose `outline_color` is essentially the `Void charcoal #060909` this section reserves for silhouette separation, and it has to hold that separation against its own Amber Gold aura and `PointLight2D`; 1.375x is conservative against section 9's Freestyle precedent of `OUTLINE_THICKNESS_CAMP 2.7` over `OUTLINE_THICKNESS_DEFAULT 1.45`, which is 1.86x. The nodes take 1.4, and that one is derived rather than judged: a four-tap cross delivers `width` pixels on an axis-aligned edge and `width / sqrt(2)` on a 45-degree one, so 1.4 is the smallest width whose ring never falls under a logical pixel anywhere on the boundary, at 1.4 x 0.707 = 0.99. It is sized to be seen rather than to match the contour family, because it is an identity rim and not a contour.

No existing gate could judge any of that. `world_render_validation.tscn` roots a `Node2D` over `YSortWorld/WorldMap2D` and contains neither the Base Core nor a resource node, so not one of its twenty-five captures holds a pixel of this material. A temporary probe put the four in-world wearers at their shipped scales on a luma-50 ground under a 0.38 camera and wrote each frame twice with `outline_width` forced to zero on the second pass, isolating the ring by difference with no threshold at all - the same instrument section 9 used on the contact shadows, retired the same way the moment it answered. Two capture facts had to be handled first and both are new. **Pausing the SceneTree does not stop shader `TIME`**, so an animated material keeps moving between the two passes and puts its own pulse into the difference; `pulse_speed` is set to zero on every toon material first, pinning `pulse` at 0.76, and every other shader-material CanvasItem in the frame - the aura, the scale glow - is hidden because those still read `TIME`. And a windowed run renders at `window_width_override` rather than at the logical viewport: 960 by 540, exactly 2x, so ring geometry is measured there and halved rather than on the LANCZOS downsample the gate writes, which would blur the very quantity being measured.

Three estimators were run on that difference and two of them were wrong, which is a fresh instance of section 7's rule rather than a nuisance. A scan-line median of leftmost-ring to leftmost-body is integer-quantised at 4 and 3 physical pixels and cannot separate 2.2 from 2.0 or 1.4 from 1.5. Ring area over body perimeter returned 1.683, 1.706, 1.725 and 1.679 for four subjects authored at 2.2, 1.4, 1.4 and 1.4 - **a constant answer across a varied population is a broken estimator**, and that is the second time it has been the diagnosis here. It assumes the ring is perimeter times thickness with no overlap and full development, and the camp's silhouette of tent, crates, mast, sandbag ring and interior holes violates exactly that, since adjacent ring segments merge and narrow holes saturate. Per the rule the camp is the subject that violates the assumption, so the estimator is discarded for the camp rather than averaged into the answer. The repair keeps only scan lines where the body's outer edge moves at most one pixel between neighbours, which is precisely the geometry the shader's x and y taps address.

The camp's body denominator in that probe read 76.5 logical pixels wide against the 69.9 its alpha chain predicts, and chasing the 6.6 was worth more than the ring measurement it does not affect. Two separate things were inside the mask and neither is the camp. The width came back to 69.5 the moment the mask threshold rose from 3 to 8 and held at 69.0 from there to 40, so the outermost seven pixels are a shallow gradient rather than a body: it is `AmberLight` spilling onto the ground, and a mask cut 5 luma off the ground cannot tell that from a sprite. The height was the more useful one. The mask ran 69.0 tall against a predicted 58.4, and profiling it by row found a 101-pixel band at rows 318 to 327 that is strongly green - mean delta +28 red, +70 green, +4 blue - bracketed above and below by two dark rows. That is the Base Core's own `HealthBar2D`, on screen because section 7 records the Base as the only owner that passes `hide_at_full = false`, and it is not hidden by the probe because it carries no material to hide it by. So this is section 7's own already-recorded lesson arriving a second time - **a metric that includes a feature drawn outside the subject is measuring that feature** - and the accident is a first delivered-pixel confirmation of the refuge bar's authored size: 64 world units through the instance's `Vector2(2, 2)` and the 0.38 camera predicts 48.6 logical pixels, the band measures 50.5 including its outline, and against a 69.0-pixel camp that is 73 percent, which is the "seventy percent of the camp" section 7 authored it to.

One scope note belongs with this. `StartMenu.tscn` draws the survivor bakes into two `TextureRect`s carrying no `material` line at all, so this material never touches the character-select cards, and section 9 records that `PlayerAvatar` ships the baked directional actor and reaches `heikki.tscn` and `shane.tscn` only through `force_legacy_presentation`. Two of the six wearers are therefore on a recovery path rather than a shipping one. They were retuned anyway, because a recovery path that renders to a different line weight than the thing it recovers is a second defect waiting for the day it is needed. `SMOKE OK (207 checks)` and `RESOURCE SCATTER VALIDATION OK (72 checks)` pass across the change, and neither shader now steps by `TEXTURE_PIXEL_SIZE` anywhere but in the comment prose that records why it used to.

The icons inside those rims had their own delivery defect, and it is the first in this document that a 1× capture overstates rather than misses. Each resource scene draws a 128-pixel SVG raster through `Sprite2D.scale 0.46` under the 0.38 camera, which is a 22.4-pixel texture footprint and a body of about 19 logical pixels: a 5.7x minification, mip level 2.54, roughly one source texel in thirty-two reaching a logical pixel. The three `.svg.import` files shipped `mipmaps/generate=false` and the `Icon` node inherited a plain linear filter, so a bilinear fetch read four of those thirty-two texels and the other twenty-eight fell between samples - the definition of aliasing, and it moved with the node as it crossed sub-pixel phases under the camera. The wood master compounded it: its two `#FFD700` straps were 3-pixel dashed strokes at 0.9 opacity and its hub ring a 3-pixel stroke, which is 0.52 logical pixels of the one colour section 4 reserves for Wood, and measured on delivered pixels it held 11.2 pixels of anchor hue against Metal's 71.9 and Tech's 69.9. The straps are now 7-pixel continuous strokes and the ring a 6-pixel stroke around a radius-10 hub, redrawn in the vector master rather than in a raster, and the three `.svg.import` files generate mipmaps with the `Icon` node on `TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS`. The 128-pixel master is unchanged as the texture source. The three scenes, `runtime_export_dependencies.tscn` and the path list in `smoke_test.gd` are the only consumers of those SVGs; `GameHUD` draws its own 12-pixel icons and is untouched, and `ResourceNodeView` reads `_base_icon_scale` off the node at ready, so its 0.84 harvest shrink and 1.12 hit pop are relative and follow any scene change.

It was confirmed on delivered pixels through the Mobile/Vulkan path with a temporary probe - retired the moment it answered - that put each node on a luma-50 ground at eight sub-pixel phases of one logical pixel with `pulse_speed`, `outline_width` and `emission_strength` pinned to zero and its light hidden, so the icon is the only thing in the crop. The anchor widening lands where the model put it, 19.6 percent against a predicted 20, and the shipped shimmer was worse than modelled, 13 to 17 percent against 7 to 14. Mipmaps remove it outright and cost the silhouette its ink: the fractional level takes 54 percent of its sample from the 16-pixel mip, where a 9-pixel stroke is 1.1 texels, so the outline ring falls from 90 to 98 pixels to 18 to 20 and the darkest decile of the body rises from 3 to 6 luma to 23 to 25. A raster of the same master at 24 pixels keeps 2.2 to 3.9x that ink with equal anchors and shimmer 12 to 30x under the defect, and at the 0.84 harvest shrink it holds 29 to 32 ink pixels against trilinear's 8 to 9 at 0.9 to 2.6 percent shimmer. It was the better icon at 1× and it does not ship, because 1× is not where the icon is delivered. **Texture minification is a physical-pixel quantity under `canvas_items` stretch: the canvas renders at the panel's resolution, so a 1080-by-2400 phone draws this icon into 89.6 physical pixels, where the 128-pixel master is minified 1.43x at mip level 0.51 and a 24-pixel raster would be magnified 3.7x into soft blocks.** A 1× capture minifies a texture four times harder than that phone does, so a shimmer or anchor-survival finding taken at 1× is a worst-case bound rather than the shipped condition - the opposite of a contour authored through `fwidth` and `stretch`, which is a logical quantity for which 1× is exact, and the reason the two families in this section are judged at different scales. Re-run at a simulated 4x panel - a 1920-by-1080 `SubViewport` under a 1.52 camera - the shipped-before and shipping paths are the same icon: anchor 16.1 against 16.3, 23.1 against 23.7 and 24.6 against 25.0 percent, darkest decile identical to the tenth of a luma at 6.3, 3.4 and 3.7, ink down 7 to 9 percent, shimmer down 2 points. The mipmaps cost that 7 to 9 percent at device scale and nothing else, and the wood strap that measured half a logical pixel before the redraw would have delivered at 2.1 physical pixels there - thin, present, and still under section 9's 1× rule, which this document keeps because the desktop gates are taken at 1× and every judgement in it has to be reproducible on them. Two probe facts outlive the question. A `SubViewport` sized to 480 by 270 with its own `Camera2D` returns true 1× pixels whatever the window is, so nothing has to be halved or downsampled - the earlier probes in this section measured at 960 by 540 and divided. And a windowed run whose script fails to parse - here an untyped `for` iterator under `untyped_declaration = 2` - leaves the Godot window open and the command hanging rather than exiting, so it has to be killed by process before anything is re-run. `RESOURCE SCATTER VALIDATION OK (72)` and `SMOKE OK (207)` pass across the change; the two `radial_light` SVGs in `assets/2d/effects/` import without mipmaps too and are left that way on content rather than on measurement, because aliasing needs detail finer than the sample grid and a four-stop radial gradient over a 128-pixel radius has none - the texels a minified fetch skips are within a luma of the ones it reads.

The same material's grade was solved in the same sitting and against the same twenty-three bakes, because a contour weight and the value it separates are one readability problem. The complaint was that props read as flat cutouts pasted onto the terrain, and the measurement agreed in the strongest possible form: with the finish as shipped, a forest prop's body p10 sat at 55.00 against a modal forest ground of 50, and an urban prop's at 54.67 against 60. The darkest tenth of a prop's own body was *brighter* than the ground it stands on in the forest, and four of the eight forest families and three of the fifteen urban families had no dark end below their ground at all. An object with no value below its own terrain has no contact and no mass; it is a decal. `_get_environment_finish` now ships contrast 1.30 against the previous 1.03 and 0.99, shadow_tint 0.24 explicitly rather than inheriting the shader's 0.12, and shadow_lift 0.025 against 0.095 and 0.110, which takes the forest step to 19.0 luma and the urban to 35.9 and leaves two and one family respectively still inverted.

Three things about how that was picked are worth more than the values. 1.30 is the knee rather than the optimum: below it each contrast step buys 3.5 to 4.2 luma of body-to-ground separation and above it 1.0 to 1.2, and the uniform's own `hint_range` ends at 1.50, so taking the last 20 percent of the available separation would have spent all the headroom a later correction needs. The search was guarded rather than maximised - body p90 within 4 luma of shipped so highlights are not crushed, local standard deviation at least the shipped value so interior modelling is not flattened, and a crush share under 2 percent - and 297 of 360 forest rows and 322 of 360 urban rows survive those guards, so the guards are shaping the answer rather than merely permitting it. And the first sweep was thrown away outright: every winning row sat at the maximum contrast of the grid with lift at its minimum and tint at its extreme, which is a search reporting its own boundary rather than an optimum. **An optimum sitting on every boundary of a grid is a broken search; print the per-axis marginal before believing a winner.**

The lift term is where that grid was lying, and unpicking it corrected a piece of arithmetic that had already been written into a comment twice. A body pixel whose graded luma clips to zero does not ship black: it exits at `cool_shadow * shadow_tint`, plus the lift direction at full weight - the lift is scaled by alpha and a body pixel's alpha is 1.0 - all of it lerped toward `cool_shadow` by `palette_cohesion`. That is one fixed value per parameter set, and nine of the twenty-three bakes report exactly it as their body p10, which is the recorded signature of an estimator that has stopped measuring its subjects. At the shipped tint of 0.24 and cohesion 0.10 it is 7.89 luma at lift 0.000 and 12.74 at 0.025, and at the sweep's own tint of 0.28 it is 8.79 at lift 0.000 - every one of them reproduced to two decimals by the closed form, and the tint has to be quoted alongside the lift because it moves the same number. Lift therefore raises the floor rather than holding anything off it, and 0.025 ships because 7.89 sits under the 8.4 this section reserves for Void charcoal outlines and cavities while 12.74 is clear of it. **A floor derived at one parameter value is not the floor at another: the lift term vanishes from the algebra at lift 0 and dominates it at lift 0.025.** The grade decision itself is unaffected, because it was taken against lift-0 rows where the two forms agree exactly - but the crush guard is not. It counts pixels under the Void charcoal luma of 8.4, so at any parameter set whose floor is above 8.4 it reads 0.00 percent on exactly the rows where nine families are pinned to that floor. **A guard set at a contract anchor can sit just below the floor it was meant to catch, so it needs a value-independent companion; here that is a count of pixels sitting exactly on the computed floor.**

The obvious follow-through was to put the player's own buildings on that same grade, since `PlacedStructure2D` overrides only `accent_color`, `accent_strength`, `sheen_strength` and `outline_width` and inherits the shader's 1.09 contrast, 0.12 tint and 0.07 lift - a divergence the retune had just widened. It is recorded here as a negative result, and the two repairs it took to get a number worth trusting are the transferable half. The first probe returned a body p10 constant to within 0.04 luma across eight different structures, which is the floor above rather than a measurement. The second, once the downsample was modelled, returned a different constant near 6.1 - which is the outline ring's own luma of 6.16. The ring is composited *outside* the source alpha and is never graded, so a mask taken from the composited alpha is measuring the contour, and at delivered size the contour is a large share of the sprite. **A metric that includes a feature drawn outside the subject is measuring that feature; at delivered size the mask has to be downsampled from the source alpha through the same filter as the colour, not read off the composite.**

Modelling that downsample is what reframed the question. A structure is a 320-pixel bake delivered through `scale 0.32` under `zoom 0.38`, which is 0.1216 - an 8.2x reduction - so the Freestyle interior line work that pinned every source-resolution percentile to the floor never reaches a player at all. The environment props deliver between 0.44x and 4.5x, which is exactly why the world sweep survived being measured at source resolution and the structure probe did not. **A bake measured at source resolution when it delivers at one eighth is measuring line work no player sees.** Measured at delivered size with a source-alpha mask, against ground anchors of 50 in the forest and 60 in the urban districts:

The premise was that the player's buildings are undergraded and flat beside a newly contrasty world, and the measurement reverses it. Their shadow and highlight ends are already in family with the environment - 31.95 against 32.07 and 130.69 against 144.35 - which the two parameter sets reach by different routes because the structure bakes are intrinsically darker sources than the environment atlases. Adopting the world grade would take their median to 39.41, forty-one luma below the environment's and well under both grounds, making the things the player builds the darkest objects in the frame. So the structures keep their inherited defaults. What the measurement appeared to point at was a real defect and a different one - their median mass sitting twenty-five luma below the environment's, which would be an exposure complaint rather than a contrast one - and the paragraph below withdraws that reading. What survives it is the separately recorded footprint problem: a delivered body of 201 to 426 pixels against the environment's 1,416 to 198,443. Neither was judged on `structure_visual_gameplay_scale.png` as it stands, because that gate renders onto a luma-22 ground while the game's are 50 and 60. **A near-black capture background cannot test a dark subject either, not only a dark shadow** - the same rule this document already records for contact shadows, hitting a second subject for the same reason.

The exposure half is now a second negative result beside the contrast one, and it fell to the fact that its comparison had no reference population behind it. The environment row in that table is a mean over six subjects. Measured over all twenty-three entries the sprite finish actually dresses, at the source resolution this section already sanctions for that family, the body median runs from 17.97 on the old tyre to 147.50 on the concrete barrier - an 8.21x spread - while the shipped grade's palette-anchor position spans 30.27x and its highlight-gate share runs from 0.00 percent to 63.66 percent with four families at or under a tenth of a percent. Section 7's own rule about the contact-shadow constant applies unchanged: a metric whose spread across a cast is 2.7x is measuring something other than what it claims to, and this one is 8.21x. Against twenty-three-subject means of 26.45 / 69.72 / 129.82 the structures' 31.95 / 54.95 / 130.69 sit above the family's dark end and level with its bright one; seventeen of the twenty-three are darker at p10 and eleven carry a body median below the structures' own. The player's buildings are median-typical of the world rather than dark beside it. Nor are they dark against the ground: the criterion that licensed the world retune two paragraphs above is body p10 against the modal terrain, it bought 19.0 luma in the forest and 35.9 in the urban districts, and the structures already deliver 18.05 and 28.05 with no change at all. The recorded six-subject row stays in the table as the number the decision was taken against and is flagged here as not reproducible on either basis - the delivered six give 29.43 / 66.42 / 135.51 and the source twenty-three give 26.45 / 69.72 / 129.82 - which does not move the conclusion, because all three agree the family median is 66 to 80 and the spread is five- to eightfold. **A family mean taken over a population with 8.21x spread cannot establish that a second family sits below it.**

Three findings closed the remaining routes into that change. The last criterion that did not depend on an environment mean was internal to the shader: `accent_color` reaches a pixel through a highlight term gated at `smoothstep(0.52, 0.96, luminance)` and a palette anchor driven by `smoothstep(0.16, 0.84, luminance)`, both evaluated on the ungraded source, so a body dark enough would simply not receive its identity accent and section 11's redundancy requirement would be doing the arguing rather than a taste. Measured, the structures' mean highlight weight is 0.0024 against the environment's 0.0020, because `PlacedStructure2D` sets `accent_strength` to 0.09 against the environment's 0.065 and that more than compensates for the darker source; two of the eight structures gate at zero, and so do two of the environment families that ship. **A uniform that compensates for a darker source can carry a subject past a criterion its raw values would fail, so read the parameter before concluding from the pixels.** The deficit itself is in the bakes rather than in the grade - the ungraded structure sources sit about twenty-four luma under the ungraded environment atlases - so any correction belongs to `generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` and not to a uniform. And a flat pre-grade gain is the wrong operator for it in any case: reaching the environment median needs a gain near 1.95, which multiplies an already-correct p90 by the same factor and takes it to about 205 with three percent of the body clipping. The midtone uniform this thread was heading toward is therefore not added, and the structures keep their inherited defaults on measurement rather than on inertia.

Two instrument notes came out of the same work and both outlive the question. The offline record grades at source resolution and then downsamples, while the GPU filters the texture first and grades the filtered texel once per screen pixel - grade-then-downsample against downsample-then-grade. At the shipped structure parameters the two agree to about 1.1 luma at p50 and 1.5 at p90, which is why the table above stands, but under a source-side gamma of 1.35 they diverge by up to 5 luma on a single subject. **A source-side operator's predicted effect is model-dependent at roughly the magnitude of one 0.05 gamma step, so an operator tuned that finely cannot be chosen on offline evidence alone.** And the delivered-size probe masks its statistics on the *composited* alpha, so its structure p10 pins at 6.2 - the ungraded outline ring's own luma of 6.16 - which is the defect recorded a few paragraphs above arriving in a third instrument of the same thread. The corrected form masks on the source alpha downsampled through the same filter as the colour, and it reproduces this section's recorded structure row exactly, which is what licensed everything measured against it.

Every CanvasItem shader under `shaders/` carried one defect that none of the paragraphs above could see, because every measurement in them was taken through it. A `canvas_item` `fragment()` enters with `COLOR` already equal to `texture(TEXTURE, UV)` multiplied by the vertex colour - the node's modulate and self-modulate - so the idiom `vec4 source = texture(TEXTURE, UV) * COLOR;` multiplies the fetch by itself, and every texel and its alpha shipped as its own per-channel square. Seven shaders had it - `sleek_sprite_finish`, `sleek_canvas_grade`, `toon_emissive_outline`, `surface_weathering`, `projectile_glow`, `solid_white_flash` and `base_scale_glow` - which between them dress every environment prop, every placed structure, the refuge, the three resource nodes, both legacy survivor scenes, the ambient and background scenery, the three weathered surface families, the projectile tracer and the hit flash. Every wearer was gamma-2.0 darkened before any grade ran. It was measured on the GPU rather than argued: a shader whose whole fragment is `COLOR = texture(TEXTURE, UV)` renders byte-identical to no material at all, and the product renders as the per-channel square. The fix is `vec4 source = COLOR;` in all seven, and each keeps the old line in its comment prose so the idiom is not reintroduced by copying a fragment header. **A shader's entry `COLOR` is already the textured, modulated fragment; a fetch multiplied back into it is a square, and a grade tuned through a square is tuned for a source the player never sees.**

Two consequences run back through the paragraphs above. Modulate now folds into entry `COLOR` once rather than twice, so a runtime tint is a plain multiply again and can only lose value - which is exactly why `WorldObstacle2D.WILD_TREE_TINT` could correct the wild canopy's hue and could not touch the contact puck section 9 records, because the puck is baked below the tint's reach. And the sprite finish's crush floor is unchanged in algebra but now sits under a source twice as bright, so the delivered minimum of a forest prop wearing the finish pins at luma 13.0 on the 12.74 closed-form floor; the body-p10-against-ground retune above was recorded at the time as decided against squared sources and owed a re-measurement rather than a re-derivation, because its guards were shaped on the right criterion; the two paragraphs below are that re-measurement, and the first thing it corrected was the attribution. The canopy-hue paragraph after the palette table was likewise measured through the squared finish and is superseded by the wild-family record in section 9.

That re-measurement is done, and its first finding is about the record rather than the render. The offline model that chose contrast 1.30 loads each atlas PNG as linear values on zero to one and never squares them, so the sweep was taken against the unsquared source the GPU only began shipping after the fix; what was wrong for the whole period was the delivered pixel and not the decision, and the sentence above attributing the decision to squared sources is corrected accordingly. The check was then made on the GPU rather than trusted from the argument. A temporary probe put all twenty-three finish wearers at their shipped scales under a 0.38 camera on their own modal grounds - 50 forest, 60 urban - with `WorldObstacle2D._get_environment_finish` read live rather than transcribed, swept the material's `contrast` over the sweep's own eight values, and wrote each subject once more with no material on a transparent viewport, so the body mask is the filtered source alpha above 0.9 and never the composite. It was retired the moment it answered. On the twenty subjects the model knows, delivered body p10 agrees with the offline prediction to within 0.0 to 2.3 luma in the forest and 0.0 to 4.3 in the urban districts, the GPU landing slightly brighter on most, which is the grade-then-downsample against downsample-then-grade difference this section already sizes.
