# Art log: Blender-to-Godot art pipeline

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md section 9 on 2026-09-25. This is the measurement record behind the contract: how each shipped value was found, what was refuted, and which instruments lied first. CLAUDE.md keeps the current contract and the rules learned; this file keeps the evidence. Numbers here are as recorded at the time and are not refreshed - read live values out of the source.

`tools/art/generate_character_sprites.py` creates a dedicated `BESPREN_SPRITE_STAGE` scene in Blender and leaves the user’s default scene intact. It builds both survivors from reproducible primitives, applies PBR materials and emission, adds three-point lighting plus Freestyle outlines, then renders 320×320 transparent PNGs into `assets/2d/characters/`. `tools/art/generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` uses the same isolated-scene policy to render the camp refuge and eight mechanically distinct structures into `assets/2d/structures/`, including the violet-triggered Razor Snare. Both scripts resolve the project root from their own location.

The render camera is orthographic and elevated at a top-down three-quarter angle. Heikki receives broader shoulders, rust tank mass, and a Gold chest slash; Shane receives a slimmer torso, asymmetrical pauldron, Cyan tech cell, and antenna. These differences deliberately survive desaturation and small-display viewing rather than relying on hue alone.

Those differences were authored and then measured, and for a long time they were not there. At the size the game actually delivers - a 320 pixel bake drawn through `Sprite.scale = 0.27` under a `zoom = 0.38` camera, so 32.8 screen pixels - Heikki and Shane were near-identical blobs separated only by hue, which is precisely the failure CLAUDE.md 11 forbids when it requires identity to be redundant across hue, silhouette, iconography, and placement. The band measurements said so plainly: Heikki led Shane in none of the six horizontal bands, trailing by 2.3 screen pixels at the head and 1.0 at the shoulders, with a centred silhouette IoU of 0.861 between two characters whose whole design premise is opposed silhouettes.

Broadening Heikki did not fix it, and the reason is the load-bearing lesson of the episode. Three successive widenings moved the shoulder band the right way and cost him symmetry every time, monotonically - mirror-IoU 0.851, 0.823, 0.806, 0.785, 0.770 - which reads as an art problem and is not one. The camera sits at `(4.25, -6.5, 7.3)` looking at `(0, 0, 1.45)`, giving camera-right `(0.837, 0.547, 0)` and camera-up `(-0.329, 0.504, 0.799)` at 68.8 pixels per world unit. Because up carries a -0.329 X component, a world-symmetric shoulder pair at plus and minus X separates in screen *Y* as well as screen X: at X 0.68 that is 0.448 units of vertical offset, so the shoulder line projected as a 21 degree diagonal. Every unit of width given to Heikki was a unit of diagonal, and "broad" and "bilaterally symmetric" were anti-correlated by the projection rather than by the art. A best-fit-axis mirror search over offsets and tilts was run first to rule out a metric artifact; it settled at 1 to 3 degrees and barely moved the numbers, because the body's near-axis mass dominates the union. The fix is `_face_camera`, which yaws the finished body by the camera's own azimuth, `atan2(-6.5, 4.25) + 90 = 33.18` degrees, mapping body +X onto camera-right and the chest onto the lens. Heikki's best-fit mirror axis then snaps to offset 0.0 and tilt 0.0 degrees, his symmetry goes to 0.998 against Shane's 0.924, and the asymmetry gap holds constant while he grows - width and asymmetry are finally independent channels rather than one budget spent twice. **The bake camera basis is authored geometry: a body built on an axis the camera does not share pays for it in projection, and the basis belongs in the record beside the model.**

The turn costs whatever was authored against the spine's back face, which is why `_FLANK_PROPS` exists: those objects are rotated back afterwards so they keep the screen position they were authored at. It recovered Shane's antenna and tech cell outright, at a full 255 delta. It did not recover Heikki's rust tank - that diff came back at max delta 4, which is bounce light and nothing else, because the occluder is `ArmR` at X 0.62 to 1.04 rather than the torso. Pairing the tanks at plus and minus 0.80 did not help either, at max delta 9 across 34 delivered pixels: present in the file, invisible on screen. Clearing both arm and pauldron laterally would need X beyond 1.45, an absurd outboard boom, so the mass moved *up* instead, to a mirrored pair at plus and minus 0.84 rising to Z 3.10 over a connecting yoke. That is worth stating as a rule rather than a fix, because the same measurement decided both halves: **the shipped tank was worth 47 delivered pixels at full opacity, so it was load-bearing and could not simply be dropped; the relocated pair is worth 2,693 bake pixels, and it lands in the head band, which was the one band still inverted.** Heikki now leads at the head by 4.6 screen pixels and at the shoulders by 3.2, the centred IoU falls from 0.861 to 0.785, and Shane is byte-identical to his pre-tank bake at max delta 0.

Making that mass visible exposed something that had been hiding behind it. `BSP_Rust` is used at exactly one site, and its 98 previously visible pixels were only ever the tank's shadow-side edge, measuring saturation 0.63; the lit material is 0.96 against the `Rust bloom #BC431A` anchor's 0.86, at hue 23.7 against the anchor's 15.2. A large mass off its own anchor is a different defect from a sliver off it, so the base colour moved from `(0.38, 0.105, 0.025)` to `(0.38, 0.089, 0.042)`, which renders at hue 18.1 and saturation 0.85. The solve is recorded because guessing at it wasted two bakes: in the red-yellow sector saturation depends only on `b/r` and hue only on `(g-b)/(r-b)`, so hitting the anchor needs blue *up* and green *down*, and raising both - the intuitive move - walks hue and saturation in the same direction and can reach neither. The regrade also corrected a metric rather than only a colour. The 20-to-70-degree band this document's separation figure calls "gold" contains rust at 23.7 degrees, so the tank had been counted as Heikki's identity accent all along; splitting the warm mass at 30 degrees, which cleanly divides the rust family from the `#FFD45A`/`#FFB52E` gold family, shows gold leading rust 2.34 to 1 at delivered scale against 3.49 to 1 before. Gold remains dominant, so CLAUDE.md 2's "Gold utility accents" holds while the rust reads as the mass CLAUDE.md 9 calls it. **A hue-band metric that straddles two anchors reports one of them as the other; check the band edges against the anchors before trusting a separation figure.**

Two procedural notes came out of the same work. A judgement made at bake resolution was wrong and the delivered-scale test reversed it: at 3x magnification the turned bodies read as flat front elevations with balloon shoulders and the shipped pose looked better, and at 33 pixels that verdict inverted completely, because the objection was about detail no player sees. This document already requires new art to be inspected at 1x logical size; the rule earns its place by having bitten in both directions now. And when a bake reports zero renders, re-run it unfiltered - a `grep` for `Saved:` hides a Python syntax error completely, which cost a cycle here.

The change is a pixel change to `heikki_topdown.png` and `shane_topdown.png`, and both are consumed by `scenes/characters/heikki.tscn`, `scenes/characters/shane.tscn`, and `scenes/ui/StartMenu.tscn` - a narrower reach than that list makes it look, and the two paragraphs after this one are about how much narrower. The official rebake matched the accepted variant at max per-channel delta 0 across all 102,400 pixels of both files, satisfying the determinism contract below. The survivor `HealthBar2D.bar_size` and `ActorGroundShadow2D.base_radius` are both flat authored values shared by the two survivors rather than alpha-derived like the Base bar, so a wider Heikki moved no gate-derived bound - and that is a note, not an endorsement: the contact shadow now spans 54 percent of his boot band against 60 percent before. That reading stood as a note for exactly as long as it took to ask what the literal behind it was anchored to; section 7 now carries the answer and the corrected radius.

Where those two PNGs actually ship is the correction this section owes, because an audit was run against them on the assumption that they are the gameplay survivors and they are not. `player_avatar.gd` says it outright - shipping defaults to the baked directional actor - so `GameWorld` draws `assets/2d/actors/scenes/hero_heikki.tscn` and reaches `scenes/characters/heikki.tscn` only through `force_legacy_presentation`, the recovery escape hatch for a malformed bake. The one place a player sees these renders in a shipping build is `StartMenu.tscn`, whose two character-select cards draw the 320-pixel bake into a 70x71 `TextureRect` at `stretch_mode = 5`. The engine proved that before the reasoning did, and the method is worth more than the anecdote: swapping both bakes and recapturing `player_presentation_live_day.png` moved 381 pixels, every one of them in the two Heikki columns at mid-torso y 99 to 104, while Shane - whose bake had just lost 1,632 alpha pixels - came back byte identical. A byte-identical frame is a diagnosis, and what it diagnosed here was the auditor. Confirm that the shipped path draws an asset before measuring the asset.

What the audit found is real and shipped anyway. `_GroundSilhouette` was a charcoal ellipsoid of half-extents (0.92, 0.62, 0.10) built as each survivor's baked contact, and a smooth ellipsoid in a three-point rig catches the key light: it owned 2,012 pixels at 88 percent fully opaque with a mean luma of 64.5 against a whole-body mean of 75.4 and the game ground's 22.5 - two fully opaque wings flanking the boots, warm olive (79, 75, 56) on one side and cool grey (91, 104, 100) on the other. A lit primitive cannot be a shadow; a shadow is composited rather than modelled, which is the whole reason `ActorGroundShadow2D` is a sibling node and not geometry. At the delivered portrait size it read as a grey puddle wider than the boots and brighter than them, so the primitive is gone, the alpha bounds are unchanged either side of the removal, and the official rebake reproduced the accepted variant at max per-channel delta 0 across all 102,400 pixels of both files.

Nothing replaces it, and that is a measurement rather than a preference. The portrait plate composites to luma 5.7 - `Color(0.008, 0.022, 0.02, 0.92)` over the card's `Color(0.028, 0.071, 0.064, 0.97)` - so a contact shadow drawn there would have under six luma of headroom to darken into, against the 22.5 ground where `GroundShadow` has real range. The character-select card is the one surface in the project where a contact shadow has nowhere to go, and the answer there is a clean silhouette rather than a darker lozenge.

Two per-asset knobs in `generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` are worth stating because one was kept and one was tried, measured, and thrown away. Freestyle line thickness is authored per asset and stays that way - `OUTLINE_THICKNESS_DEFAULT = 1.45` against `OUTLINE_THICKNESS_CAMP = 2.7`, carried as the fourth column of the render list - because a silhouette read at the 480×270 target needs a heavier line on the camp than on a structure rendered at a tighter ortho scale. A per-asset column for the shared teal rim's energy was added on the same reasoning and then removed. The complaint driving it was that the tent's broad canvas facet looked cyan, which would have painted the largest surface of the refuge in Shane's identity colour against CLAUDE.md 2; cutting the rim from 820 W to 165 W did not move it and recolouring it to neutral grey did not either. Measuring the bake explained why: six perceptibly green pixels in 26,950 - 0.022 percent, all dark, all inside one 8 by 15 patch - and the facet itself measures (121, 120, 49), which is red over green. It is a yellow highlight on a lit canvas slope. The metric that started the whole thread tested `g > r` by a single unit, which also flags `BSP_Structure_Steel (0.27, 0.31, 0.29)` and `BSP_Camp_Stone (0.115, 0.125, 0.120)` - both authored a hair green while reading as neutral grey, both faithful to the locked `Worn steel #596360` anchor. `COOL_RIM_ENERGY` is therefore a single shared constant again, and the script carries the episode as a standing instruction: measure the bake first, with a threshold that means something.

The character generator has now been through that same argument and come out the other way, which is only defensible because the measurement was different. Judge a bake at the size it is delivered rather than the size it is rendered: `heikki.tscn` scales the 320-pixel sprite by 0.27 and the gameplay camera ships at zoom 0.38, so a survivor draws into 32.8 pixels of canvas and measures 13.8 by 22.5 pixels of actual body. Two candidate defects died on that number. A bright right-shoulder wash - 261 pixels on Heikki and 583 on Shane, ninety-five percent of it right of centre - resolves under connected components into one coherent blob per character, 21 by 23 and 26 by 27, which is two screen pixels square. Cutting `BSP_CoolRim` from 980 W to 280 W removes a third of it while costing sixteen percent of whole-body luma and a third of the cool footprint, and raising accent roughness to soften the specular core made Shane worse rather than better, 583 pixels to 805, because a wider lobe puts more pixels over a fixed brightness threshold even as peak value falls from 1.000 to 0.976. Downsampled to the 33 pixels that actually ship, the roughness variants differ from the shipped bake by a mean of 0.20 to 0.82 out of 255. The shoulder is closed as a negative result.

Rendering the survivors at that true size instead surfaced the defect worth fixing, and it is hue rather than brightness. Measured on delivered pixels across the chromatic body, Heikki was 61.0 percent Gold against 32.5 percent cool and Shane 55.6 percent cool against 38.7 percent Gold - a third of Heikki's chroma sitting in Shane's identity family, and the pair separated by 61 against 39 percent Gold rather than by Gold against Cyan. Section 2 asks these two to read as complementary silhouettes before equipment is noticed and section 11 asks identity to stay redundant across hue, silhouette, iconography and placement; at 14 by 23 pixels the iconography is subpixel and the authored silhouette difference is fifteen to eighteen percent, so hue was carrying nearly the whole load and carrying it badly. The cause is structural: the three-point rig is built once outside the per-character render loop, so one rim light wears Shane's Cyan across both bodies. Energy cannot resolve that, because the cut that helps Heikki hurts Shane - his cool share falls 55.6 to 46.2 - and recolouring the shared rim to Copper Patina only trades one collision for another, taking Shane to 43.5. `RIM_COLOR_HEIKKI` and `RIM_COLOR_SHANE` therefore split the rim per character at unchanged energy, so no luma is paid: Heikki gets a desaturated cool grey and Shane keeps his Cyan exactly. Heikki reads 96.7 percent Gold against 2.2 percent cool, separation rises from 22.3 to 58.0 points, and his body luma goes up rather than down, 0.2841 to 0.2869. The warm-cool modelling contrast survives where it belongs: his low-chroma population grows from 4.2 to 26.3 percent of the body and turns genuinely cool, mean blue-minus-red -0.020 to +0.010, while the chroma that remains goes decisively warm, -0.089 to -0.219. Temperature is form; chroma is identity.

That is the same knob the camp script had already tried and deleted, so the difference has to be stated rather than assumed. There the complaint was six perceptibly green pixels in 26,950 and the knob went because the measurement refuted the premise. Here the measurement is taken at delivered scale, finds 74 of 228 chromatic pixels in the wrong family, and the accepted fix moves 157 of 1,089 delivered pixels. The standing instruction did not change - measure the bake first, with a threshold that means something - it was followed, and this time it returned a defect. The same rebake also exercised the determinism contract stated below: three separate Blender invocations produced Shane byte-identical every time and the official bake reproduced both sweep renders at a maximum per-channel delta of exactly 0, while Heikki differs from his predecessor on 13,060 pixels. Unmodified source reproduced exactly and the one intended change showed up as a change. Accent emission belongs beside this for the same reason `COOL_RIM_ENERGY` does: it is the AgX property pulled the other way. The survivors' accent emission strengths were reduced from 2.4 and 3.0 to 0.4 and 0.5, because a transform that desaturates monotonically with brightness lets an over-driven identity accent burn off the very hue that makes it an identity accent.

That gap is now closed. `write_generated_asset_manifest.py` used to name `generate_camp_and_structure_sprites.py` as its single generator and cover exactly the nine camp and structure renders, so the character family - the other half of what `tools/art/` bakes - carried no provenance record at all, which is the worse form of the failure its own docstring warns about: not a manifest gone stale but a manifest never written, against which `smoke_test.gd` could only assert that the paths exist. It now carries a `FAMILIES` table and writes one manifest per family, each still listing its renders rather than globbing them so a stray PNG cannot enter one and a missing render fails loudly instead of shrinking it. `assets/2d/characters/generated_asset_manifest.json` joined the smoke gate's required files, which is why the headless count now reads 207 rather than 206.

The camp does keep its own warm firelight, and its isolation is structural rather than a per-light toggle. The render loop hides every asset collection except the one being rendered, and a light parented inside a hidden collection contributes nothing - so the camp's rig cannot reach the other eight renders, and no separate light bookkeeping has to stay correct for that to hold.

Two properties of the AgX Medium High Contrast view transform shape how these bakes are judged. It desaturates monotonically with brightness, so removing a large fill light reads as both darker and *more* saturated - a change that looks like two edits when it is one, and which has misled a bake review here before. Consequently a bake is compared against the previous PNG rather than against an expectation of what removing a light should do. `tools/art/write_generated_asset_manifest.py` regenerates the manifest for this whole generated family and must be re-run after any rebake, since nothing else notices that a PNG changed.

That manifest used to record "EEVEE is not bit deterministic", and the correction is a contract rather than a footnote. Three consecutive bakes of an unmodified generator do produce three different file digests - but at an identical byte count, and decoding them shows byte-identical IDAT streams with a max per-channel delta of exactly 0 across all 102,400 pixels. Only the `tEXt` `Date` and `RenderTime` chunks differ, because Blender stamps the wall clock and the measured render duration outside the pixel data. The old wording licensed the wrong conclusion - it made a rebake whose pixels had genuinely drifted read as expected noise. A rebake of unmodified source must match the shipped pixels exactly, and any non-zero delta is a regression to explain rather than absorb. The recorded per-file `sha256` therefore identifies the file that shipped rather than one a rerun reproduces byte for byte, and `generator_sha256` pins the source.

The animated actor family is baked through the same offline discipline. `tools/art/run_actor_bake.py` declares the roster as the game's actual cast — the two survivors and the eight `EnemyAgent2D` variants — and `build_actor_body.py` authors each body directly onto the CC0 KayKit skeleton so a goliath and a crawler differ in mass rather than in tint. `bake_animated_actor.py` renders every clip through one orthographic camera, lighting rig, and palette, laying frames out as `rows = 8 compass headings, columns = frames`; `pack_actor_sheets.py` downsamples them on the host interpreter and records each sheet's cell size, content bounds, per-row variation, and `ground_offset` — the projected world origin, derived from the camera framing rather than measured from alpha — into `sheets/sheet_manifest.json`. `tools/asset_pipeline/build_actor_sprite_frames.gd` then writes one `SpriteFrames` and one `AnimatedSprite2D` scene per actor, pinning each sprite to that recorded origin, and logs clips, animation counts, and foot offsets in `actor_frames_manifest.json`. Sheets import with VRAM compression and `process/fix_alpha_border`, so Android ships only the `.etc2.ctex` variant with the cutout edge intact. `ActorFacing` selects a row by dot product against the baked headings at runtime; no armature, mesh, or `Node3D` ships.

That `ground_offset` is faithful to its own definition and was still the wrong number for one job. It is where the bake camera projected the world origin, which makes it the correct pin for the sprite - but the point a body's pixels actually touch the ground is not that origin, because in a three-quarter view a limb reaching toward the lens projects below it. `ActorGroundShadow2D` was drawn at the origin, so the contact ellipse sat behind the feet it was meant to be under. Measured against the sheets' own alpha over all eight headings and every idle frame, the ellipse covered 2.4 percent of the crawler pack's foot pixels, 8.9 percent of the plague carrier's and 4.4 percent of the splitter's: those three were standing above their own smudge entirely. `actor_frames_manifest.json` calls the same number `foot_offset`, which is the name that made this easy to miss - it says feet and means origin.

The proof that the gap is systematic rather than stance variation is the axis that was not corrected. Per heading the foot-band centroid swings 10 to 19 sheet pixels horizontally - walker heading 2 at -12.5, goliath at -18.9 - and averages between -0.44 and -2.21, which is a planted foot orbiting the body's centre as it turns. Vertically it is positive on 78 of 80 heading readings and averages between +1.79 and +13.73. Horizontal is centred and swinging; vertical is displaced and constant, and that asymmetry is the whole argument.

`EnemyPresentationDefinition.contact_offset` carries the correction per actor in sheet pixels, which at `EnemyPresentationCatalog.ACTOR_SCALE` are screen pixels. The survivors take theirs as the scene's `Shadow.position` of `Vector2(0, 12.75)`, in world units rather than sheet pixels because that node is a sibling of the scaled sprite rather than a child of it, so 8.5 sheet pixels arrive already multiplied by `BAKED_ACTOR_VISUAL_SCALE`. The enemy "before" column is the ellipse at the origin; the survivors' is their previously shipped offset rather than zero, and both facts are why the table exists. `enemy_scrap_shield` measures 0.979 already and takes exactly zero, so no global constant could ever have been correct - one shared nudge would have pushed the only actor that was right off its own feet. And the survivors' scene shipped a hand-authored `Vector2(0, 10)` worth 0.715 and 0.776, so this change retires a magic number rather than introducing one.

The delivered pixels were checked separately, because a sheet measurement is a claim about what should reach the screen rather than a reading of what did. Two temporary probes put the cast on the review capture's own olive ground at scale 1.0, paused the tree, and wrote each frame twice with every shadow branch hidden on the second pass, so the contact is isolated by difference rather than by a luma threshold that cannot tell a shadow from a boot outline. The survivor probe's own foot-band centroid - an estimator that does not depend on `base_radius` at all - lands at +12.75 world units for Heikki and +13.28 for Shane against a shipped +12.75, while its coverage argmax sits at +12.25 for both: two independent estimators bracketing the shipped value. Both probes are retired; they are review instruments and not gates, and the frames they wrote would go stale the moment a sheet moved.

Those same probes show that the offset is only half the defect. Across the eight baked headings the survivors' own lowest pixel moves between 9 and 20 world units below the origin, an 11-unit spread against a contact ellipse whose vertical half-height is 7.4, so no single offset can reach both ends and the worst heading stays low by construction. `ActorGroundShadow2D.base_radius` was `marker_radius * SHADOW_FOOTPRINT_SCALE`, and `marker_radius` is a readability marker ring rather than a measurement of the body. That was the open half of this, it was a radius problem rather than an offset one, and section 7 now carries the per-variant measurement that closed it.

The finding was already in the codebase one class over. `GroundShadow`'s own docstring records that 20 of the 21 imported visual families had their whole contact behind their own pixels, and concludes that contacts have to be anchored to the sprite the camera actually sees. The baked actors are the family that conclusion missed, because they arrive with an authored pivot and an authored pivot looks like an answer. **A pipeline value that is correct for the job it was derived for will be reused for a job it was never measured against; check what a constant describes before matching another value to it.** Three method notes came with it. Pick the centre of a coverage plateau rather than its peak, because an argmax with a steep side is a value that only works for the frame it was measured on. A single rendered frame samples one of eight baked headings and it will be the worst one often enough to look like a regression, so cross-check a delivered number against the matched sheet cell before believing it. And an estimator that does not depend on the suspect constant is worth running even when the one that does is kept: the centroid agreed on sign and magnitude everywhere and lost on coverage, which is why the coverage value shipped with corroboration rather than on its own.

Runtime SVGs remain vector masters so they can be edited without lossy round trips, but Godot imports them as textures for scene use. New art must follow `type_subject_variant_state` naming, keep masters under `assets/2d/`, and be inspected at 1× logical size. A vector master delivered through a `Sprite2D` is also delivered through a texture filter, and section 8 records what the three resource icons cost when theirs was wrong: a 128-pixel raster read without mipmaps at a 5.7x minification aliases on every sub-pixel move, mipmaps fix it, and the 1× rule here is a worst-case bound for a texture rather than the shipped condition, because minification is a physical-pixel quantity under `canvas_items` stretch.

Poly Haven contributes the CC0 nature/prop half of the environment atlas system through `tools/art/blender/bespren_map_asset_workshop.blend`, not through runtime 3D. The Blender/Poly Haven MCP workflow imported the reviewed CC0 1k GLTF sources `tree_stump_01`, `dead_tree_trunk`, `rock_moss_set_01`, `metal_trash_can`, `concrete_road_barrier`, and `old_tyre` into that dedicated Blender 5 workshop. `tools/art/render_polyhaven_environment_sprites.py` normalizes their unrelated scan lighting with one orthographic camera, transparent film, Freestyle silhouettes, AgX midtones, and a shared three-light rig, yielding twelve 384×384 RGBA renders. `tools/asset_pipeline/build_polyhaven_environment_atlas.gd` then packs them in fixed order into one 1536×1152 atlas and records alpha bounds, padded atlas regions, CC0 URLs, source IDs, and SHA-256 provenance in `polyhaven_asset_manifest.json`.

At runtime, `WorldObstacle2D` lazily creates cropped `AtlasTexture` views for six rock variants, an occasional stump, the dead trunk, clean/rusted trash cans, a tyre, and selected concrete roadblocks. The sprites use linear mipmaps plus shared forest/urban instances of `sleek_sprite_finish.gdshader` to constrain saturation, cool deep shadows, add a restrained accent sheen, and preserve a dark silhouette at the 480×270 target. They replace presentation only: the existing `StaticBody2D` collision, flow mask, stable seeds, obstacle ordering, and peer-one movement resolution stay canonical. The workshop is excluded from Godot scanning with `.gdignore`; no Poly Haven GLTF, mesh, PBR material, or Node3D enters the shipping game scene.

The complementary local environment pipeline bakes 29 reviewed Kenney City Builder and Atomic Realm Post-Apocalyptic GLBs through `tools/art/blender/bespren_local_environment_workshop.blend`. `render_local_environment_sprites.py` produces sixteen coherent RGBA frames, and `build_local_environment_atlas.gd` fills one 4×4, 1536×1536 atlas with four city ruins, mall/industrial shells, separate west/east houses, a vehicle, barricade, pole, salvage, roadside barrier, and three camp satellites: bedding, supply cache, and medical cache. The manifest records source, license, workshop, renderer, luminance, alpha-bound, and output hashes. Runtime city, Ostari, village, wreck, fence, pole, and camp-satellite visuals consume that atlas while retaining canonical collision and flow footprints. Long Ostari shells keep a full-footprint foundation beneath four baked modules and salvage dressing; west/east houses use separate frames and top-down silhouette correction. Atomic Realm-derived images remain embedded in the game and may not be redistributed as an asset pack.

The village fences carried a repetition defect the atlas could not have caused, and the first fix for it looked right and was not. Each of the twelve village fences - four horizontal 1500-unit runs and two vertical 1700-unit runs per village - is drawn by `_install_local_fence_visuals` as four segments cut from `LOCAL_WOOD_BARRICADE_REGION`, and every segment shipped the same frame at the same orientation, so a fence read as one stamp repeated four times at equal gaps: on the west village capture, three segment runs 33 to 34 pixels wide at gaps of 19, 21 and 20. The frame is strongly asymmetric - mirror IoU 0.48 over its own alpha, mean absolute luma difference against its own mirror 41.08 - so a horizontal flip gives a segment a genuinely different silhouette at no atlas cost, and at the village capture scale a segment's self-mirror MAD is 20 to 21.5 luma against 5 to 9 between two same-orientation neighbours. That pair of numbers is the estimator the whole change is judged on, because it is what caught the first fix. That fix flipped each segment on an independent seeded coin, `_unit_noise(noise_base) < 0.5`, and a per-segment coin leaves all four segments identical on one fence in eight and three identical neighbours on half of them; a probe over the twelve fences found exactly that - `WestVillageFence_03` at 0,0,0,0 and `EastVillageFence_02` at 1,1,1,1 - and the 1x mirror test on the visible west fence returned direct MAD 5.3, 6.6 and 9.4 against mirror 20.7 to 21.5 on every pair, which is three same-orientation segments.
